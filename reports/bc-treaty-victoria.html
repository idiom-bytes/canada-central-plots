Victoria's Fine Print

Source: Ministry of Indigenous Relations and Reconciliation, "TTA Treaty Area and Treaty Lands" map (June 2026) · BC Public Accounts 2025/26, Note 28 · Times Colonist, CHEK News, Victoria News, The Epoch Times (2026)

In August 2026, a viral social-media map titled "Songhees Nation Proposed Treaty Lands" — showing Hatley Castle, downtown Victoria parking lots, and island reserves circled in Crown-land yellow — was presented as part of the same 135,425-hectare provincial commitment covered in Chapter 1. It isn't, exactly. The map is a real, primary-source government document: the BC Ministry of Indigenous Relations and Reconciliation's own June 2026 treaty-area poster for the Te'mexw Treaty Association's five-Nation table (Malahat, Scia'new, Snaw-naw-as, Songhees, T'Sou-ke). But of the nine parcels named across its Victoria-area panels, only four are actual new provincial Crown land commitments of the kind Note 28 counts: Esquimalt 113, Enterprise Wharf, the Q Lot (assessed at $24.8 million per the Times Colonist), and a strip of Discovery Island Marine Provincial Park. Hatley Park — the map's single largest, most visually dramatic parcel — is federal land owned by the Department of National Defence, entirely outside Note 28's provincial scope, and is now the subject of a separate, unresolved civil claim by Esquimalt Nation. "New Songhees 1A" and the Chatham/Discovery Island reserve parcels are land Songhees already holds; PCC Pandora is private land Songhees already owns outright. Two different documents, two different legal scopes — conflated into one number.

Who is accountable for this map?

Ministry of Indigenous Relations and Reconciliation Published the map
Confirmed as the actual primary source of the viral "Songhees Nation Proposed Treaty Lands" map: PDF metadata shows it was produced in Adobe Illustrator and created June 10, 2026, as page 6 of a 7-page "TTA Treaty Area and Treaty Lands" poster series covering all five Te'mexw member Nations. It is not a Songhees Nation or advocacy-group document — it is a provincial government treaty-negotiation map, later re-illustrated and shared by news outlets and on social media.

Te'mexw Treaty Association The five-Nation table
The umbrella body for Malahat, Scia'new (Beecher Bay), Snaw-naw-as, Songhees and T'Sou-ke filed its Statement of Intent to negotiate on July 15, 1994. It reached an Agreement-in-Principle on April 10, 2015 (approximately 1,565 ha of Crown land, not including existing Indian Reserves) and is now in Stage 5 of the BC Treaty Commission's 6-stage process. Each of the five Nations will hold its own membership ratification vote on its own final treaty text before this reaches Stage 6 (Implementation).

Department of National Defence / Government of Canada Owns Hatley Park
Hatley Park — Hatley Castle and the Royal Roads University campus it sits on — is federal land, approximately 229 hectares, owned by the Department of National Defence. It sits entirely outside Note 28's provincial Crown land scope, but it is a real, actively negotiated line item at the Songhees treaty table, not map background: multiple 2026 news reports describe it as proposed for transfer from Canada to Songhees Nation once the treaty is ratified by Songhees membership.

Esquimalt Nation Contests the transfer
A distinct First Nation from Songhees — both are successor communities of the Lekwungen people, split into separate Indian Act bands in the late 1800s — Esquimalt Nation filed a civil claim on March 27, 2026 seeking a permanent injunction against designating Hatley Park as Songhees treaty land, and asserting its own Aboriginal title claim over the site. As of August 12, 2026, the claim's outcome has not been reported. This report treats Hatley Park's ultimate disposition as an open legal question, not a settled outcome either way.

"Public access and recreational opportunities for the provincial parks would still be safeguarded under the terms of the treaties." — British Columbia's stated position, as reported by The Epoch Times, Aug 11–12, 2026 (the province's position as reported, not an independently verified legal guarantee)
