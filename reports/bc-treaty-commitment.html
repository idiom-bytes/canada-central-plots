The Commitment

Source: BC Public Accounts 2021/22–2025/26, Note 28 · BC Treaty Commission Final Agreement fact sheets

Every year, deep in the fine print of British Columbia's Public Accounts, the province discloses a running list: how much Crown land it has committed to First Nations under active treaty negotiations. As of the 2025/26 fiscal year, that list totals 135,425 hectares — about 334,600 acres — across 20 First Nations and treaty tables. That number went viral in August 2026. What usually gets left out: the total has actually fallen for two straight years from its 2023/24 peak, and it is dwarfed by the 232,746 hectares already transferred under BC's four completed, in-effect modern treaties. This chapter is the ledger itself — verified line by line against the original government documents.

Who is accountable for this number?

BC Ministry of Finance Discloses the figure
Publishes the commitment every year in the Public Accounts as a contingent contractual obligation — Note 28 has appeared, in the same location, for at least the last five fiscal years checked (2021/22–2025/26). This is the accounting record, not the negotiation itself.

Ministry of Indigenous Relations and Reconciliation Negotiates the land
The provincial ministry at the table for every one of the 20 nations/tables below, and the publisher of the treaty-area maps (see Chapter 2) that put specific parcels on the record.

Government of Canada Funds the money, not the land
Note 28's own text is explicit: "the capital transfer components for all treaty agreements will be, in most cases, entirely provided by Canada." The land is provincial Crown land; the cash that typically accompanies a settlement is a federal cost. Reporting that treats this as one undifferentiated "cost to BC" gets the split backwards.

"It is expected the capital transfer components for all treaty agreements will be, in most cases, entirely provided by Canada. The current commitments of provincial Crown land for all Final Agreement and Incremental Treaty Agreement tables are as follows..." — BC Public Accounts 2025/26, Note 28

BC Treaty Commission Independent facilitator
Oversees the 6-stage negotiation process all 20 tables sit within. A "Final Agreement" is a completed, ratified treaty; an "Incremental Treaty Agreement" is a separate, legally binding pre-treaty land transfer that can happen years before — or even without — a Final Agreement. Note 28 combines both categories into one total, which this report keeps distinct where it matters.
