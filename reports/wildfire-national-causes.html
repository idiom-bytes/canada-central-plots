Canada Is Letting Itself Burn › Chapter 2

It's Not Just BC

Source: Canadian National Fire Database (NFDB), Canadian Forest Service / NRCan · 448,602 fires, all provinces & territories, 1930–2025

The Monitoring Gap chapter found BC's own records showing a cause-recording cliff in 1999–2000. But BC isn't even the province with the most fire: the Northwest Territories and Saskatchewan each burn more area than BC most years. This chapter goes to the Canadian National Fire Database — the one dataset with consistent, per-fire cause coding (Human / Lightning / Unknown) across every province and territory — to ask the same question nationally. It finds something starker than BC's cliff: Manitoba's fire-cause records show 100% “Unknown” for four straight years, 2000–2003, and Yukon has an almost-identical, still-unresolved gap happening right now (2022–2025).

The verdict

Section 10

Is BC the exception, or the rule? We checked.

NFDB's own copy of BC's cause data was wrong — badly. This report doesn't just disclose that and move on: it corrects BC's numbers everywhere on this page using BC's own authoritative data, and it asks the obvious next question — is any OTHER province's NFDB entry also wrong?

What we found for BC, and what we did about it

We cross-checked the three other provinces with an independent source of their own

Two territories where we checked and the answer is "unknowable" — not "unknown"

The Northwest Territories and Yukon both publish their own current per-fire wildfire datasets. We checked both directly for a cause field. Neither one has one — not "unknown," not blank, the field doesn't exist at all in the source agency's own data model. That means NFDB's NT/YT cause coding (including the Yukon unknown-cause spike in Section 3) can't be independently confirmed OR refuted at the source. We're flagging that distinction explicitly rather than lumping it in with provinces where we simply couldn't find a dataset.

What we could NOT check at all

No independent per-fire cause dataset — of any kind, current or otherwise — was found for Saskatchewan (its own GIS service is currently offline), Manitoba (publishes annual aggregate counts only, no per-fire records), New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island (annual aggregate counts only), or Newfoundland & Labrador. Their figures throughout this report rely on NFDB alone. The report card below marks every row's verification status explicitly — hover the "Verified" column for the specific reason in each case — rather than implying every number carries the same confidence.
