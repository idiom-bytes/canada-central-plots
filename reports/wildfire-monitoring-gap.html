The Monitoring Gap

Source: BC Wildfire Service (live incident records, 1950–2026) · Statistics Canada · federal union & government reporting, 2026

In 2026, France's Interior Ministry announced 420 arrests, including 166 minors, for deliberately or accidentally starting wildfires — a rolling, published tally. Canada has no equivalent: no federal or provincial agency publishes an annual count of wildfire-arson arrests. This report goes to the primary source — BC Wildfire Service's own incident-by-incident records, over 200,000 fires back to 1950 — to ask what the real trend actually is. It finds something more concrete than an arrest count: the share of BC wildfires with no recorded cause at all was stable at 4–14% every year through 1998, then jumped to 38.2% in 1999 and 50.8% in 2000 and stayed elevated for over a decade. At the same time, federal agencies responsible for wildfire coordination, mapping, and weather monitoring saw budget and staffing cuts in 2026, and the specialized aircraft that fight these fires from the air didn't grow to match the worsening seasons either — for its own, separate reason. Each section below walks through one piece of that story with the data behind it.

Section 1

Who tracks this, and what got smaller?

BC Wildfire Service keeps unusually good incident-level records — every fire back to 1950 is tagged Person, Lightning, or Unknown. That data shows the real gap isn't "too much arson" — it's that Canada's capacity to even record a cause, and to coordinate/monitor the response once a fire starts, has been inconsistent for decades and was cut further in 2026.

Government Operations Centre (Public Safety Canada)

Federal Disputed cut Canada's 24/7 emergency-coordination centre for all-hazard events, including wildfires. In January 2026, around 60 of its roughly 110–130 staff received workforce-adjustment letters.

"Gutting it by almost half." — Sharon DeSousa, National President, Public Service Alliance of Canada (PSAC) · Global News, 2026-07-23

The Emergency Preparedness Minister's office disputes the scale, saying the centre's core workforce fell by only 10 people, from 110 to 100. Both figures come from the same cut, tied to the federal government's Comprehensive Expenditure Review targeting $60B in savings — timed to land during one of the most intense wildfire seasons in Canadian history.

Natural Resources Canada — satellite wildfire mapping

Federal At least 17 remote-sensing staff, who produce federal satellite-based wildfire mapping, received workforce-adjustment notices in the same round of cuts. The union representing them raised the mapping-capacity risk directly.

Cuts "raise questions about Canada's future wildfire-mapping capacity." — Stéphanie Fréchette, VP, Professional Institute of the Public Service of Canada (PIPSC) · The Hill Times, 2026-07-22

Environment and Climate Change Canada — weather monitoring

Federal ECCC disbanded its weather radar research team and ended the 230-transmitter Weatheradio public alert network in March 2026, also under the Expenditure Review. Both feed into fire-weather risk prediction. (A separate, broader claim that Canada's weather-station count has fallen by half since the 1980s could not be independently verified from a primary source and is not used as a figure in this report.)

So how much did any of this actually save?

Enforcement is case-by-case, not systematized

No Canadian agency — federal, provincial, or RCMP — publishes an annual aggregate count of wildfire-arson arrests or charges. What exists is individual prosecutions, reported one at a time:

    Alberta RCMP put it plainly in June 2025: they said they weren't aware of any arson charges connected to that season's wildfires — not because there was no fire activity, but because no one was centrally counting.

    Smoothing the noise: the gap by decade

    Section 7

    Did firefighting capacity keep up? The water bombers

    If wildfires have been a growing problem since at least 1999, did Canada's aerial firefighting fleet and funding grow to match it? The honest answer is nuanced: suppression spending rose roughly in step with area burned — but the specialized amphibious water-bomber fleet stagnated, and not primarily because governments withheld money.
