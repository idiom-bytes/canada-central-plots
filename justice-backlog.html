A System That Can't Keep Up

When courts cannot process cases fast enough, two things happen: people pile up in jail before any trial, and serious charges get stayed for delay. Canada's own correctional data shows the first directly — most people now in provincial jails have not been convicted of anything.

What the data shows

This is the measurable half of a backed-up system. The other half is invisible in custody data: under the Supreme Court's R. v. Jordan ceilings (18 months in provincial court, 30 in superior court), cases that exceed the limit can be stayed for unreasonable delay — the charges simply dropped. A jammed court produces both outcomes at once: the accused who are detained wait longer, and the cases that time out are released without trial.

Reading the data: figures are average daily counts of adults in provincial/territorial custody (most of the system; federal inmates are all sentenced and excluded). "Remand" is the official term for pre-trial detention. National total reported by StatCan as "Provinces and Territories".
