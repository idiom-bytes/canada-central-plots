Could It Happen Here? Grooming Gangs, Britain & Canada's Data

The Origin Data Gap: Canada Doesn't Track Who Commits These Crimes

We built a decade-long database of 3,342 distinct Canadian sexual-exploitation, human-trafficking and child-exploitation cases (2016–2026). Then we asked what the official statistics cannot answer: the offender's immigration status or country of origin. The result — 99.4% of cases give no indication at all. Statistics Canada's Uniform Crime Reporting Survey records no such field, rendering any link to immigration unmeasurable by design. A democracy cannot debate what it refuses to count.

Permanent vs. Temporary: Canada's Immigration Volumes

Canada admits two very different streams. Permanent immigration reached 435k in 2024/25, but the temporary-resident population grew far faster — now 2.56M, nearly doubling (+1.2M) since 2021. Work-permit holders alone (1.30M) outnumber the entire annual permanent intake several times over. Net non-permanent residents peaked at +781k in a single year (2023/24) before the cap forced a reversal. Direct from Statistics Canada.

● The Big Picture — June 2026

The Asylum Channel: The Stream That Won't Close

Ottawa says immigration is being cut — and permanent residents, work permits and study permits have all fallen from their 2024–25 peaks. One stream is the exception: asylum. New claims rose +608% since 2015 to 113,040 in 2025, and 525,000 claimants are now in the country. It is also the one category where Canada publishes country of origin (2025 leaders: Haiti, India, Iran, Nigeria, Mexico). The accumulation is the face of a system that admits far faster than it resolves or removes. This view connects all five investigations end-to-end.

Catch & Release — June 2026

A System That Can't Keep Up: Remand & the Court Backlog

More than three in four people in Canada's provincial jails today have not been convicted of anything — they are held on remand, awaiting trials the courts cannot deliver in time. The un-convicted share rose from 22.5% in 1978 to 76.6% in 2023/24, becoming the majority around 2004/05. The remand population has grown +538%, even as the sentenced population shrank. The flip side: cases that exceed the R. v. Jordan ceilings are stayed for delay.

A Nation That Can't Feed Its Children

1 in 4 Canadians — nearly 10 million people — cannot reliably afford to eat. 1 in 3 children lives in a food-insecure household. Food bank visits have doubled since 2019, yet food banks only reach 12% of the hungry. In Nunavut, 58% of households are food insecure. This is not a supply problem — it is a policy failure.

Supply Chain — May 2026

Canada’s Heavy-Duty Truck Certification Gap

The U.S. moved heavy-duty truck emissions certifications from the EPA to NHTSA. Canada’s import regulations still cite the EPA. The gap is administrative — the technical standards are aligned — but the practical result is that Canadian dealers may not be able to order 2027-model trucks unless the regulation is updated before August 2026. ~20,000 Class 8 trucks per year flow through this supply relationship.
