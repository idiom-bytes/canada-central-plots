## Correcting the viral claim

**"$1.28 trillion in minerals" and "the Tahltan land transfer" are two separately-sourced facts
that have been merged into one claim.** This is the single most important finding in this
investigation and the reason it exists.

1. **The $1.28 trillion figure** is a British Columbia Geological Survey (BCGS) estimate of
   total in-situ contained-metal value for the entire "Golden Triangle" mining district of
   northwestern BC — published in Information Circular 2022-05, *"The Golden Triangle of
   northwestern British Columbia"* (8 pages), based on compliant reserves/resources as of
   Dec 31, 2021 and commodity spot prices as of April 25, 2022. It is a **Canadian-dollar**
   figure (**$1,280 billion CAD ≈ US$985 billion** at the report's own exchange rate), includes
   low-confidence *Inferred* resources (inflating the headline number), is not net of
   extraction/permitting costs, and is **not a land value or money changing hands**. A full-text
   search of the 8-page circular returns **zero** mentions of "Tahltan," "First Nation," or
   "Indigenous."
   Primary source: https://cmscontent.nrs.gov.bc.ca/geoscience/PublicationCatalogue/InformationCircular/BCGS_IC2022-05.pdf

2. **"The Tahltan land transfer"** refers to a still-**unsigned**, **not publicly released**
   "Foundation Agreement" between the Tahltan Central Government (TCG) and the Province of BC,
   revealed only through a Freedom of Information request by the Public Land Use Society
   (PLUS). It would recognize Tahltan Aboriginal Title and Rights, restructure governance, and
   involve land transfers across the **≈95,933 km² (11% of BC)** territory Tahltan asserts —
   but it is a **territorial claim, not an itemized parcel transfer**: no acreage figure for
   actual land to change hands is public anywhere, and PLUS itself did not obtain any draft
   text (400+ pages of related government records were withheld from disclosure). Internal
   government target was Fall 2025 finalization; that target was missed. Status as of Aug 12,
   2026: **in negotiation, unsigned, not public.**
   Primary source: https://publiclanduse.ca/news/plus-foi-records-reveal-bc-negotiating-tahltan-title-land-transfers-and-governance-framework-across-11-of-province/ (Warren Mirko, PLUS, May 19, 2026)

3. **How the two got merged:** Northern Beat (Fran Yanor & Warren Mirko, Jul 3, 2026) is the
   earliest identified reporting to combine the two facts, stating Tahltan's claim "covers 70
   per cent of the Golden Triangle... which the BC Geological Survey estimated... to hold a
   total contained metal value of C$1.28 trillion" — correctly attributing the figure and
   framing it as a territorial overlap, not a completed transaction.
   https://northernbeat.ca/news/backed-by-ottawa-bc-negotiates-governance-negotiates-aboriginal-title-in-mineral-rich-northwest/
   The most direct source of "hand over" framing — the language most likely to have produced
   the viral claim as commonly repeated — is the **BC Conservative Party**'s conservativebc.ca
   post, published **August 11, 2026**: *"the NDP plans to hand over up to 96,000 square
   kilometres (23.7 million acres, larger than the country of Portugal) of northwest B.C.—and
   $1.28 trillion in mineral wealth—to the Tahltan Nation."* This is **partisan advocacy
   framing of a negotiation still in progress**, not a government or Tahltan statement, and it
   bundles Tahltan together with several other Nations (Kitsumkalum, Kitselas, Wuikinuxv,
   In-SHUCK-ch, Te'mexw).
   https://conservativebc.ca/massive-ndp-land-transfers-could-total-up-to-24-million-acres/

**Not independently verified:** the "70% overlap" figure itself (repeated consistently across
secondary sources, no underlying GIS/boundary document located).

## Background

Tahltan territory sits in the "Golden Triangle" mineral belt of northwestern BC, centered on
Dease Lake, Telegraph Creek, and Iskut. Unlike most BC First Nations engaged in title/rights
negotiations, **Tahltan is not part of the BC Treaty Commission process** and does not appear
in BC Public Accounts 2025/26 Note 28's treaty-land table. Its relationship with the Province
runs on a separate track: a sequence of escalating negotiated instruments (below) rather than a
single treaty, alongside six major mines/projects operated by seven different resource
companies within its asserted territory, plus a documented case of a former minister's
post-office move to a mining-company board.

The operative Tahltan-side legal entities are the **Tahltan Central Government (TCG)**, the
**Tahltan Band Council**, and the **Iskut Band Council** — no source in the underlying research
used "Tahltan First Nation" as a distinct legal entity.

## Revolving Door

### Bill Bennett — the single documented finding in this investigation

Bill Bennett, as BC's Minister of Energy and Mines, personally signed the province's
foundational **2017 bilateral BC-Tahltan agreement on Red Chris Mine** (with TCG President Chad
Day). https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2017MEM0001-000128

After leaving cabinet/his MLA seat, he:

- Joined the board of **Eagle Plains Resources (TSXV: EPL)** as a director just **three days**
  after leaving office.
  https://www.eagleplains.com/news/eagle-plains-announces-appointment-bill-bennett-board-directors-resignation-ron-netolitzky
- Became a director of **Kutcho Copper Corp.**, whose Kutcho copper-zinc project is located
  ≈100 km east of Dease Lake **within Tahltan Territory**, and which signed its own
  exploration/communication agreements with TCG in 2018.
  https://kutcho.ca/news/2018/kutcho-copper-signs-exploration-and-communication-agreements-with-tahltan-central-government/
- Became a director of **Ascot Resources Ltd.** (Premier gold project, Nisga'a Treaty lands near
  Stewart — adjacent to, not within, core Tahltan territory).
- Became a consultant to the board of **Rare Earth Ridge Resources Corp.** (Mount Major Hart
  claims, ≈93 km ENE of Dease Lake, same NW-BC mineral belt).
- His LinkedIn profile describes his post-political career as "consulting services and
  corporate board participation in the energy & mines space."
  https://www.linkedin.com/in/kootenaybillbennett/

As minister at the time of BC's May 2016 Auditor General mining-compliance report (which used
Red Chris/Imperial Metals as contextual example of weak provincial oversight), Bennett did not
resign despite calls to do so.
https://www.straight.com/news/691076/bc-energy-and-mines-minister-bill-bennett-wont-resign-wake-damning-auditor-generals

**Classification: this is a documented revolving-door PATTERN, not an in-office financial
conflict.** No evidence was found of any donation, payment, or arrangement predating or during
his time as minister that would tie the 2017 agreement he signed to his later board seats. It is
reported factually, as a pattern for readers to weigh — not as an assertion of wrongdoing.

**No other named official** (Rankin, Chandra Herbert, Brar, Davidson, Heyman, Cullen, Hartwell,
Alty, Chartrand, Ross) has a documented prior-employment, consulting, or post-office board
relationship with Red Chris/Newmont, Galore Creek/Teck/Newmont, Schaft Creek/Teck/Copper Fox,
Skeena Resources, or Seabridge Gold, in either direction. This should be read as **"none
found," not "none exist"** — the search did not include a full BC Lobbyist Registry check,
SEDAR/SEDI insider-filing search, or FOI request for each individual.

both last modified Aug 10, 2026) are catalogued on Canada's Open Government Portal, but their
actual download files are hosted at the same blocked `lobbycanada.gc.ca/media/...` URLs. A future
pass needs either an authenticated/interactive browser session capable of passing the Cloudflare
challenge, or a request routed through a non-flagged/residential IP.

### Cross-Connection Findings

Stated plainly, per this investigation's quality bar of reporting negative findings as
explicitly as positive ones:

1. **Northern Confluence's own "Partners" page** names SkeenaWild, Salmon Beyond Borders, and
   MiningWatch Canada together (alongside the BC Mining Law Reform network) — a direct,
   documented, primary-source organizational link among three of the environmental NGOs in
   this file.
2. **Nikki Skuce (Northern Confluence) and Jamie Kneen (MiningWatch Canada) co-chair the BC
   Mining Law Reform network**, founded 2019 — described in the underlying research as "the
   single most significant documented cross-organization connection found" in the NGO network.
3. **Rivers Without Borders is a formal, dues-paying member of MiningWatch Canada's
   22-organization coalition** — the clearest institutional cross-connection found.
4. **MiningWatch Canada's involvement in Tahltan territory predates its own 2010 SCC litigation
   by five years**: it hosted the 2005 Tahltan Elders moratorium statement and covered the 2006
   Iskut blockade, and is separately documented as connected to Klabona Keepers' territory
   through that early coverage.
5. **Bill Bennett's Kutcho Copper Corp. directorship carries a Mining Association of BC board
   seat** — extending his documented revolving-door finding from one company board onto the
   province's central mining trade association.
6. **AME's and MABC's boards combined hold named seats for all six mapped companies** (plus
   Kutcho Copper) — normal trade-association structure, explicitly not framed as a conflict.
7. **Jonas Rigaux sits simultaneously on AME's board (appointed 2026) and PLUS's board** — the
   clearest documented personnel overlap found between the registered Tahltan-specific
   mineral-exploration lobbyist and the FOI/transparency organization whose reporting anchors
   this investigation's central correction.
8. **The K'adesibe Collective (documented Dec 3, 2025 by The Narwhal) is explicitly distinct
   from Klabona Keepers** — no source connects the two groups, and no continuity, overlap, or
   rebranding between them should be inferred. They are reported here as two separate,
   differently-dated grassroots opposition voices.

**Negative findings, stated explicitly:** No board, staff, or funder overlap was found between
any of the six environmental/conservation/Indigenous-advocacy NGOs (SkeenaWild, Klabona
Keepers, David Suzuki Foundation, MiningWatch Canada, Rivers Without Borders, Salmon Beyond
Borders, Northern Confluence) and any of the six mining companies, N. Murray Edwards, the
Keevil family, Bill Bennett, or any named BC/federal minister. No funding or governance overlap
was found between David Suzuki Foundation and MiningWatch Canada themselves. No individual was
found sitting on more than one of SkeenaWild/Salmon Beyond Borders/Rivers Without
Borders/Northern Confluence's boards or staff (network-level ties exist via partner listings
and coalition membership, not shared personnel). These are reported as genuine negative
findings — "not found," not "confirmed absent" — consistent with this investigation's practice
of not filling gaps with inference.
