# Tahltan Territory: Officials, Negotiators, and Legal/Judicial Record

Research pass covering government officials, negotiators, prior-employment/revolving-door
checks, political donations, litigation, and oversight findings for Tahltan Nation
territory (northwestern B.C.) land and resource-decision matters. Companion to the
Tahltan investigation stakeholder file (mining companies, ownership).

Prepared per `investigations/README.md` schema: normal exercise of a government role
(a minister signing an agreement, an MLA representing the riding) is **not** logged as a
conflict of interest. Only documented specific ties — financial stake, prior
employer/client relationship, undisclosed donation, family connection — are flagged as
such. All other involvement is reported factually as role/date/action only.

Research date: 2026-08-12. All facts below are sourced; gaps are marked explicitly
rather than inferred.

## 5. Political donations

BC banned corporate and union political donations province-wide effective **November 30,
2017** (Election Amendment Act, 2017), capping individual contributions at $1,200/year.
[BC Gov News](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2017PREM0085-001593); [Bennett Jones](https://www4.bennettjones.com/Blogs-Section/BC-Bans-Corporate-and-Union-Donations-in-Provincial-and-Municipal-Election-Campaigns)
This materially limits what a donation search post-2017 can show — mining companies
themselves cannot donate to BC parties; only individual employees/executives can, within
the $1,200 cap.

**Pre-ban era (2005–2017), general pattern — not Tahltan-specific:** Teck Resources was
the **largest single corporate donor to the BC Liberal Party**, giving more than $2.8
million to the BC Liberals between 2005–2015 (over 20x what mining companies gave to the
BC NDP in the same period), and $1.5M+ since 2008 specifically, versus about $60,000 to
the BC NDP. Teck chair **Norm Keevil** personally donated $65,585 to the BC Liberals
since 2010. [The Narwhal, "How Teck Resources Benefits..."](https://thenarwhal.ca/how-teck-resources-benefits-being-b-c-liberal-s-largest-donor/); [CCPA Corporate Mapping Project](https://www.policyalternatives.ca/news-research/5-2-million-in-political-donations-and-more-than-22-000-lobbying-contacts/)
Separately, in March 2017 it emerged that a Teck government-relations manager,
**Carleigh Whitman**, along with six other registered Teck lobbyists, had made a combined
roughly $19,000 in personal BC Liberal donations that Teck said were "listed in error"
and were actually corporate donations paid on a Teck credit card. [The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/teck-mining-lobbyist-s-donation-bc-liberals-listed-error-company-says/)

**Important caveat:** None of the sources found in this search tie these donations
specifically to Bill Bennett, to a Tahltan-territory decision, or to any named official
in this file. The Teck-BC Liberal donation pattern is a documented, general provincial
political-finance fact from the corporate-donation era, reported here for context per
the README's instruction to check donation databases — but it is **not** presented as a
Tahltan-specific conflict because no source connects it to a Tahltan decision or a named
Tahltan-file official. This is flagged as a gap: a full Elections BC FRPC database
query by individual donor name (Teck, Newmont, Seabridge Gold, Skeena Gold + Silver,
Copper Fox Metals, Eagle Plains Resources, Kutcho Copper, Ascot Resources executives,
cross-referenced against Chandra Herbert, Rankin, Bennett, Ralston, Brar, Davidson,
Heyman, Hartwell, Cullen) was not completed in this pass and would be the next step to
either confirm or rule out individual-level donations.

**Federal:** No federal donation search was completed against Elections Canada's
political financing database for Rebecca Alty, Ellis Ross, or the Liberal/Conservative
parties from named-company executives; this is a research gap, not a finding of "none."

## 6. Litigation / judges

Six matters identified touching Tahltan territory, the named mining companies, or TCG
governance. Rulings are reported factually — a court ruling against or for the Province
is not evidence a judge "facilitated" anything.

### 6.1 MiningWatch Canada v. Canada (Fisheries and Oceans), 2010 SCC 2, [2010] 1 SCR 6
- **Court:** Supreme Court of Canada.
- **Judge:** Rothstein J., writing for a unanimous Court.
- **Date:** January 21, 2010.
- **Subject:** Federal environmental-assessment scoping for Red Chris. MiningWatch
  argued Fisheries and Oceans Canada and Natural Resources Canada unlawfully narrowed
  the scope of the federal environmental assessment for Red Chris (open-pit mine and
  mill), which should have triggered a comprehensive study.
- **Outcome:** The Court found the federal authorities erred in scoping the project too
  narrowly, but declined to order further relief halting the mine's development —
  i.e., a partial/declaratory win for MiningWatch with no practical remedy affecting the
  mine. [CanLII, 2010 SCC 2](https://www.canlii.org/en/ca/scc/doc/2010/2010scc2/2010scc2.html)

### 6.2 Gitxaała v. British Columbia (Chief Gold Commissioner), 2023 BCSC 1680
- **Court:** BC Supreme Court.
- **Judge:** Justice A. Ross.
- **Date:** September 26, 2023.
- **Subject:** Challenge (by Gitxaała Nation and Ehattesaht First Nation — not Tahltan)
  to BC's "free entry" mineral claim staking system, arguing the Crown's duty to consult
  is triggered by the *registration* of a mineral claim, not only at a later permitting
  stage.
- **Outcome:** Declared the province's mineral tenure registration system breaches the
  Crown's duty to consult; declaration suspended 18 months to allow the province to
  design a consultation-compliant regime. [CanLII, 2023 BCSC 1680](https://www.canlii.org/en/bc/bcsc/doc/2023/2023bcsc1680/2023bcsc1680.html)
- **Relevance to Tahltan:** Not a Tahltan party case, but directly governs how mineral
  claims are staked across the province, including within Tahltan territory, and TCG has
  separately intervened in related mineral-tenure litigation on the general legal
  question. [Tahltan intervention document](https://tahltan.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/TAHLTAN-NATION-ASSERTS-INHERENT-RIGHTS-TO-SUCCESSFULLY-PROTECT-CULTURALLY-SENSITIVE-AREA-FROM-PROPOSED-MINING-EXPLORATION.pdf)

### 6.3 Gitxaała v. British Columbia (Chief Gold Commissioner), 2025 BCCA 430
- **Court:** BC Court of Appeal.
- **Judges:** 2–1 split decision; majority reasons by **Justice Dickson**.
- **Date:** December 5, 2025.
- **Subject:** Appeal of the above.
- **Outcome:** Majority held BC's mineral claims regime is inconsistent with Article
  32(2) of UNDRIP as incorporated into BC law via the Declaration on the Rights of
  Indigenous Peoples Act, effectively striking down the "free entry" mineral tenure
  regime as it previously operated. [Fasken](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2025/12/undrip-in-action); [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/b-c-court-of-appeal-rules-previous-free-entry-mineral-tenure-regime-inconsistent-with-undrip/)
- **Relevance to Tahltan:** Same as above — general mineral tenure law affecting all BC
  territory including Tahltan lands, not a Tahltan-initiated case.

### 6.4 Tsetsaut Skii km Lax Ha Nation v. British Columbia (Environment and Parks), 2026 BCSC 1042
- **Court:** BC Supreme Court.
- **Judge:** Justice Burke.
- **Subject:** Judicial review of the Province's July 2024 decision to grant a
  "Substantial Start Determination" for **Seabridge Gold's KSM mine** (northwestern BC,
  near Stewart), brought by the Tsetsaut Skii km Lax Ha Nation — a distinct Nation from
  the Tahltan, though KSM sits in the broader region where Tahltan, Nisga'a, and Gitxsan
  interests also overlap (Tahltan and Nisga'a formed the Treaty Creek Limited
  Partnership in 2023 around KSM-related contracting).
- **Outcome:** Court found the Province breached its constitutional duty to consult the
  Tsetsaut Skii km Lax Ha Nation before issuing the Substantial Start Determination.
  [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/province-consult-duty-gold-mine-9.7229420)
- **Relevance to Tahltan:** Not a Tahltan party case; included because it directly
  concerns one of the five companion mining companies (Seabridge Gold/KSM) named in the
  research brief.

### 6.5 Day v. Tahltan Central Government, 2025 BCSC 1363
- **Court:** BC Supreme Court (heard in Prince George).
- **Judge:** Justice Kirchner.
- **Date:** Ruling issued July 23, 2025.
- **Subject:** Internal TCG governance dispute — wrongful/constructive dismissal claim
  by former TCG President **Chad Norman Day** (president July 2014–March 2024),
  arising from his indefinite suspension by the TCG board of directors in **November
  2023** — the same month Day, as sitting TCG president, had signed the historic
  Red Chris consent-based decision-making agreement with the Province (Nov 1, 2023).
  Day was formally dismissed in March 2024.
- **Outcome:** Court found Day was constructively dismissed because the TCG board failed
  to follow its own Director Accountability Process before suspending him; awarded Day
  over $320,000 in damages. The court explicitly declined to award aggravated or
  punitive damages, finding it could not conclude the board acted in bad faith or with
  reprehensible conduct. [Prince George Citizen](https://www.princegeorgecitizen.com/local-news/judge-awards-ex-tahltan-president-more-than-320k-for-wrongful-dismissal-10965191); [HR Reporter case summary](https://www.hrreporter.com/chrr-plus/employment-law-cases/11275)
- **Relevance:** This is an internal TCG employment-law matter, not a land/resource
  rights case, and the ruling does not find or imply any wrongdoing connected to the
  Red Chris agreement itself. It is included because it involves the same individual
  (Day) who signed multiple provincial agreements on Tahltan's behalf, and because the
  timing (suspension the same month as the Red Chris consent agreement signing) may be
  of interest to readers — no source reviewed draws a causal link between the two
  events, and none should be inferred from the timing alone.

### 6.6 Tahltan Nation opposition to the original Red Chris approval (2005–2006, background)
- Tahltan Nation publicly opposed the original approval of the Red Chris mine (predating
  the cases above); this is background context for the 2010 SCC case (6.1) rather than a

**No Federal Court (Federal Court of Canada, trial division) case with a named judge
directly between Tahltan Central Government and Canada was identified in this search.**
This is reported as a gap, not as evidence no such case exists.

### 7.1 BC Auditor General — "An Audit of Compliance and Enforcement of the Mining
Sector" (May 2016)
- **Ministries examined:** Ministry of Energy and Mines and Ministry of Environment.
- **Finding:** Compliance and enforcement activities of both ministries were

- **Minister at the time:** Bill Bennett (Minister of Energy and Mines) did not resign

## 8. Summary of key findings for the investigation

1. **The one clear documented conflict-adjacent finding:** Bill Bennett, the minister who
   personally signed the province's 2017 bilateral Red Chris agreement, moved directly
   into mining-company board seats after leaving office, including a directorship at
   Kutcho Copper Corp., a company operating inside Tahltan Territory with its own TCG
   exploration agreement. This is a revolving-door pattern, not an in-office conflict —
   no evidence found of any financial arrangement, promise, or donation predating or
   during his time in office.
2. **No other revolving-door ties were found** among the current and recent ministers,
   MLA, or federal officials checked, and no evidence of donations from the five named
   mining companies' executives to these specific officials was found — but the
   donation-database search (Elections BC FRPC by individual name, Elections Canada
   political financing search) was not exhaustively completed and is flagged as the
   clearest next step.
3. **Litigation involving the named companies exists but does not touch Tahltan Central
   Government as a party** in the cases found (Gitxaała, Tsetsaut Skii km Lax Ha) except
   for the internal TCG employment case (Day v. TCG) and the 2010 MiningWatch case
   specific to Red Chris's original federal approval.
