# Tahltan Territory: Industry Association & Advocacy-Group Network — Founding, Funding, Governance, Overlap

Deeper-dive companion to `research-stakeholders.md` §4 (NGO/advocacy) and §5 (lobbyist
registry). That file already established: (1) the Association for Mineral Exploration
BC (AME) is a registered BC lobbyist specifically on Tahltan matters (two 2015-2016
registrations), and (2) the Public Land Use Society (PLUS) is the source of the FOI
reporting that revealed the unsigned "Foundation Agreement." This file does not repeat
those findings — it goes deeper on founding history, funding, boards of directors, and
cross-network overlap for AME, the Mining Association of BC (MABC), and PLUS.

Sourcing key (same convention as `research-stakeholders.md`):
- **VERIFIED** — primary source (org's own official page/filing, government registry
  record) directly confirms the claim.
- **REPORTED** — credible secondary source, or an organization's own public statement
  not independently cross-checked against a primary filing, states the claim.
- **UNVERIFIED / COULD NOT CONFIRM** — searched for and not found, or found only in
  low-confidence sources; flagged as a research gap rather than asserted.

Research date: 2026-08-12.

---

## Headline finding

**The single most newsworthy result of this pass:** the Mining Association of BC's
current board of directors (48 seats) includes a **named seat for every one of the six
companies already mapped in this investigation** — Newmont, Imperial Metals (two
seats), Teck Resources (two seats), Seabridge Gold, Skeena Resources (Skeena Gold &
Silver), and Copper Fox Metals — **plus a seat for Kutcho Copper Corp. held by Bill
Bennett**, the former BC Minister of Energy and Mines already flagged in
`research-officials-legal.md` as the investigation's clearest documented revolving-door
finding. This is the first place in this investigation where Bennett's name surfaces on
an industry-association governance body, not just an individual company board. See §2.2.

A second, distinct finding involves PLUS: one of its current directors (Jonas Rigaux)
sits simultaneously on AME's board, and PLUS's own platform has published explicitly
anti-DRIPA, "pay-to-play"/"rent-seeking" framed commentary specifically about the
Tahltan agreements — including from PLUS's own Executive Director and from its Legal &
Policy Committee chair — that goes well beyond neutral FOI-transparency reporting. This
materially affects how PLUS's "11% of the province" FOI story should be read. See §3.

## 1. Association for Mineral Exploration BC (AME)

### 1.1 Founding history

AME traces an unbroken institutional lineage to **1912**, when it was formed as the
**Vancouver Mining Club** (first president Robert R. Hedley), renamed the **Vancouver
Chamber of Mines** later that year, then the **British Columbia Chamber of Mines**
(1921), the **British Columbia & Yukon Chamber of Mines** (1945), and finally
**Association for Mineral Exploration British Columbia** in December 2000. AME's own
February 2026 announcement refers to its "114th Annual General Meeting" (held Jan 28,
2026) — consistent with a 1912 founding (2026 − 1912 = 114). **REPORTED** (Canadian
Mining & Energy trade history piece; corroborated by AME's own AGM numbering).
https://www.miningandenergy.ca/read/bcs-association-for-mineral-exploration-turns-100
https://amebc.ca/news/ame-2026-board-and-executive-announced/

### 1.2 Current governance — leadership

**Todd Stone** became AME's President & CEO effective August 5, 2025. Stone is a
**former BC Liberal/BC United MLA for Kamloops–South Thompson (2013–2024)** and served
as BC's **Minister of Transportation and Infrastructure (2013–2017)**, among other
cabinet roles; he ran fourth in the 2018 BC Liberal leadership race and did not seek
re-election in 2024. **VERIFIED** (AME's own release; corroborated by multiple BC trade
press outlets and Wikipedia).
https://amebc.ca/news/association-for-mineral-exploration-welcomes-todd-stone-as-new-president-ceo/
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Stone

This is a documented former-cabinet-minister-to-industry-association-CEO pathway,
structurally the same *type* of pattern already flagged for Bill Bennett in
`research-officials-legal.md` (minister → mining-sector board/executive role), though
Stone's ministerial portfolio (Transportation) was not Energy/Mines and he has no
documented prior role signing a Tahltan-specific agreement. Reported as a governance
fact, not as a conflict — flagged because the pattern itself (senior BC cabinet veterans
moving into mineral-sector leadership) recurs across this file's subjects (see also
Bennett at Kutcho Copper/MABC, §2.2, and Jonas Rigaux's dual AME/PLUS seat, §3.4).

### 3.2 Legal structure

PLUS describes itself in its own press material as a "**provincial non-profit**" and its
"About Us" page states it is "governed by a Board of Directors, as outlined in its
Constitution and Bylaws, filed with the **Registrar of Companies of the Province of
British Columbia**" — i.e., **PLUS is registered as a BC society, not (per this pass) as
a CRA-registered charity.** A direct search of CRA's charity-listing terminology did not
surface a CRA registration/BN number for PLUS, and no charity number is displayed on
PLUS's own site (which solicits donations via a "Donate" button but does not state
whether donations are tax-receiptable). **COULD NOT CONFIRM CRA charitable status either
way** — this matters because if PLUS is not a registered charity, its funders are under
no obligation to be disclosed via T3010 filings, meaning its funding sources are
**opaque by default absent voluntary disclosure**, which PLUS's own site does not
provide (no funder/donor list or acknowledgment section found anywhere on
publiclanduse.ca in this pass). Flagged as a material research gap given how much this
matters for assessing PLUS's own transparency, given that transparency is PLUS's central
advocacy claim about others.

**Synthesis, stated factually per this investigation's quality bar:** PLUS is not a
politically neutral government-transparency watchdog in the mold of, say, a
freedom-of-information-focused journalism nonprofit with no stated position on the
underlying policy question. Its own leadership (Mirko, its founder/ED, and Moyse, its
Legal & Policy Committee chair) have personally authored, and its platform has
republished from explicitly aligned commentators (Canadian Constitution Foundation,
Fraser Institute-adjacent framing), content that is **specifically and repeatedly
critical of the Tahltan agreements and of the DRIPA framework generally**, using
consistent "pay-to-play"/"rent-seeking"/"vote buying" framing across multiple authors
and outlets. This does not invalidate the underlying FOI documents PLUS obtained (the
existence and contents of the withheld Foundation Agreement records are a separate,
independently verifiable fact from PLUS's framing of them) — but a reader relying on
PLUS's "11% of the province" framing, its emphasis on scale ("larger than Portugal"),
and its "gradual splitting of British Columbia" language should understand this comes
from an organization with a stated institutional position that DRIPA-style
consent-based agreements are illegitimate absent court adjudication, not from a
disinterested transparency intermediary. This is a framing-context finding, not a
factual-accuracy finding — nothing here contradicts the underlying FOI facts already
recorded in `research-stakeholders.md`.
