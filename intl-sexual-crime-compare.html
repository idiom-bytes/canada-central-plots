Recorded sexual violence: Canada vs. Europe

A like-for-like attempt at the comparison the viral “Rape of Europe” chart gets wrong — using rates per 100,000 and trend shape, with every legal/recording change marked, instead of raw counts.

Read this first. Country levels are not comparable — definitions and recording rules differ enormously (Canada has no “rape” offence; England & Wales records very broadly; Poland very narrowly). Rising numbers are driven substantially by law changes and greater willingness to report, not necessarily by rising incidence. This is recorded crime, not victimization, and says nothing about offender origin.

1 · Trend shape (indexed, 2013 = 100)

Why a country can be high here but flat in the index (or vice-versa). This chart shows growth since 2013, not the actual rate. A country starting from a low base (France: 17/100k in 2013) shows huge growth yet can still end below a country that started high and grew slowly (Canada: 59→87). Indexing hides the starting level — so see the absolute rates below.

    The spread here is an artifact of definitions, not danger. England & Wales and Sweden look extreme because they record broadly; Poland looks near-zero because it records narrowly. Do not read this as “country X is N× more dangerous.”

    3 · Actual rate per 100,000 over time (absolute)

    Same definition caveat as the bar: lines sit at different heights mostly because the offences are defined differently (Canada = broad “sexual assault”; France/Germany/Poland = narrow “rape”). Compare shapes over time, not heights.

    Was Canada already high before any of this? Yes. Canada’s recorded sexual-assault rate was ~82/100,000 in 1998, fell to a low of ~57 around 2014, then climbed back to ~87 by 2024. So the recent rise is largely a return toward late-1990s levels (plus the 2017 reporting/recording effect) — not a new phenomenon, and it predates the immigration-policy period the report examines.
    What this does and doesn’t show.
    • Does: recorded sexual-violence rates rose across most of the West over the decade — Canada included — broadly tracking reporting and legal change.
    • Doesn’t: prove incidence rose, rank countries by safety, or link any trend to immigration. None of these series carry offender origin.
