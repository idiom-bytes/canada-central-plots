The Core Question · Policy vs. Crime

Did the Policy Changes Weaken the System?

This is the question the whole investigation builds toward. We place Canada's police-reported rates for human trafficking, kidnapping, sexual assault and child exploitation directly against the dated decisions — Acts, Orders in Council, regulations and court rulings — that changed how the immigration and justice systems screen, detain, prosecute and remove. The aim is to see whether system-weakening events line up with measurable harm. We are careful about what that can and cannot prove.

Method & limits (read this)

  • Correlation is not causation. That a weakening event preceded a rise in a crime rate does not prove it caused it. Reporting practices, public awareness, offence codification, policing capacity and many other factors move these rates. We show the temporal relationship; we do not assert a causal one.
  • Rates are police-reported (StatCan UCR) and undercount true prevalence; trafficking and child-exploitation offences were codified relatively recently, so we index from 2014 rather than inception.
  • "Effect" is our reading of each event's direction on system capacity, stated factually in each summary. Recent Gazette regulations are marked "unassessed" where their direction can't be judged from the citation alone.
  • The subject is decisions and institutions, not migrants. Most system-weakening events were executive instruments (no parliamentary vote) — see the Policy Timeline.
