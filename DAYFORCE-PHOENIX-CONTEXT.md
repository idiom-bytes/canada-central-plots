## Background

The Government of Canada's **Phoenix pay system** (built by IBM, launched 2016) became one of the worst public IT failures in Canadian history — **$5.1 billion spent on fixes** as of June 2025, with 233,653 unresolved pay transactions still affecting 133,619 employees as of September 2025.

**Dayforce Canada Ltd.** (formerly Ceridian Canada Ltd., renamed 2024-02-01) won the contract to replace Phoenix. The company was taken private by US private equity firm **Thoma Bravo** for US$12.3B in February 2026.

## Conflicts of Interest & Risk Flags

### 1. Revolving Door — Gianluca Cairo

Chief of Staff to PSPC Minister (Phoenix oversight) → Chief of Staff to Innovation Minister → VP Public Sector at Ceridian/Dayforce. Transitioned **July 2019**, one month after Ceridian shortlisted. Now client contact on lobbying registrations.

### 2. Revolving Door — Regan Watts

Former government staffer across **5 ministerial offices** (Finance, Foreign Affairs, Health, Transport, PMO) from 2006-2012. Now the primary contact between Dayforce and senior decision-makers (Benay, Anand, Ali).

### 3. Procurement Opacity

Three vendors shortlisted via "agile procurement" (Ceridian, SAP, Workday). **No public explanation** for why SAP and Workday were eliminated. The $350.6M contract is effectively sole-source after the initial gate process.

### 4. Cost Escalation

$16.9M (pilot, 2021) → $350.6M (build contract, 2025) → **$4.2B+ estimated total** (AG, 2026). The AG says this "does not include important costs" and will be higher. For context, the PBO estimated $2.6B in 2019.

### 5. Foreign Private Equity Ownership

Dayforce taken private by **Thoma Bravo (US)** for US$12.3B (Feb 2026), with minority stake from **Abu Dhabi Investment Authority (ADIA)**. Delisted from NYSE/TSX. Reduced transparency for a company handling payroll of 431,000 federal employees. No public Investment Canada Act national security review found.

### 6. Deloitte — Dual Involvement

Involved in the **original Phoenix business case** alongside IBM/PwC (flagged by CBC as potential conflict). Now an **implementation partner** for the Dayforce replacement.

### 7. Accelerated Timeline

Target moved from 2034 to March 2031. AG Hogan warned this could "create similar pay issues" as Phoenix. Announced **after** the audit period ended.

### 8. Unresolved Backlog

233,653 unresolved pay transactions. PSPC will **not meet** its March 2026 clearing target. Errors will carry over into Dayforce.

### 9. Pay Rule Simplification Stalled

AG: "A decade later, there has been little progress made to simplify these rules." TBS and unions have not reached consensus. PSPC building costly cloud extensions as workaround ($4M+/year).

### 10. Zero In-House Reports in 12 Months

Despite an active $4.2B+ contract, the in-house lobbying registration shows **0 communication reports in the last 12 months**. Either all lobbying is being routed through the consultant (Watts), or in-house contacts are not being reported.
