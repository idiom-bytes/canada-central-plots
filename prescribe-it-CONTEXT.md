## Background

PrescribeIT was Canada's national electronic prescribing (e-prescribing) service — designed to replace faxed and paper prescriptions with secure digital transmission from doctors to pharmacies. The program was operated by **Canada Health Infoway**, a federally incorporated not-for-profit created in 2001 by Canada's First Ministers and funded exclusively by the federal government. TELUS Health, a division of TELUS Corp., was selected as the technical solution provider in May 2017.

After more than **$250 million in federal spending** over nine years, Canada Health Infoway announced on February 12, 2026 that PrescribeIT would cease all operations on May 29, 2026. At the time of shutdown, **fewer than 5% of Canadian prescriptions** were being sent through the service — making it one of the most expensive per-outcome failures in Canadian health IT history.

The program's failure immediately triggered parliamentary accountability questions. Conservative MPs moved to compel production of the full TELUS Health contract, all payments, and IP ownership records. Liberal MPs moved to block those disclosures. The controversy goes to the heart of whether Parliament can meaningfully scrutinize arm's-length federal spending.

## Conflicts of Interest & Risk Flags

### 1. CRITICAL — Jocelyne Voisin Dual Role
**Jocelyne Voisin** is simultaneously:
- Senior Assistant Deputy Minister at Health Canada (the department that oversees and approves contributions to Infoway)
- A member of Canada Health Infoway's Board of Directors
- A Designated Public Office Holder lobbied by TELUS Health's in-house lobbyists in June 2025

This creates a direct governance conflict: she is on both sides of the funder-funded relationship, and the company whose technology is at the centre of the accountability controversy has documented meetings with her.

**Why it matters:** No independent oversight exists of this relationship. Infoway's funding renewals pass through Health Canada — the very department where Voisin is a senior official and from which she sits on Infoway's board.

### 2. HIGH — TELUS Health Lobbying Around PrescribeIT RFP
Charles Harnick (former Ontario AG) registered as a consultant lobbyist for TELUS Health Solutions GP in **September 2016** — exactly when Infoway's PrescribeIT Call for Proposals was under evaluation. His registration explicitly covered Government Procurement and Health Canada. TELUS Health won the contract in May 2017.

**Why it matters:** A former Attorney General was actively lobbying federal procurement officials on TELUS Health's behalf during the evaluation of an RFP that TELUS subsequently won.

### 3. HIGH — TELUS Retains IP from $250M Public Investment
Despite receiving over $250 million in public funds channelled through Infoway, **TELUS Health retains ownership of the proprietary transmission hub technology** that PrescribeIT was built on. The government cannot continue or redeploy the service without TELUS's consent.

**Why it matters:** Parliament has not received the IP ownership agreements despite repeated requests. The terms under which public infrastructure became private IP are unknown.

### 4. HIGH — Liberal MPs Blocking Contract Disclosure
At HESA March 24, 2026, Liberal MP **Sonia Sidhu** moved an amendment specifically to remove PrescribeIT contract records (the 2017 contract, all amendments, IP agreements, termination notice, and full payment history) from a Conservative document production motion. Before a vote could occur, a procedural adjournment prevented resolution.

**Why it matters:** Parliament has not been able to review the terms of a $250M+ contract. The specific materials blocked are precisely those needed to assess whether TELUS received favorable terms, retained IP it shouldn't have, and faces any liability for program failure.

### 5. HIGH — No Auditor General Review Since 2009
The 2009 Auditor General found retroactive contracting, inadequate funding agreements, and misleading adoption statistics. Despite $2.65+ billion more in spending since then — including the entire PrescribeIT program — no public OAG performance audit of Canada Health Infoway has occurred since 2009/2011.

**Why it matters:** The accountability gap that produced the 2009 findings went unaddressed and produced a repeat failure with PrescribeIT.

### 6. MEDIUM — Stuart Barnable Lobbying Ceased at Shutdown
TELUS Health's consultant lobbyist Stuart Barnable (H+K Strategies), a former Chief of Staff in the Senate, ended his registration **February 17, 2026** — the same month PrescribeIT's shutdown was announced. His registration covered federal-provincial health cooperation and government procurement.

**Why it matters:** The simultaneous end of lobbying and the program raises questions about whether PrescribeIT's shutdown ended TELUS's need for political access, or whether there is a relationship between the two events.

### 7. MEDIUM — Infoway Board Chair is Former Deputy Minister
Board Chair **Dr. Peter Vaughan** is a former Deputy Minister of Health. The entire board structure interweaves current and former senior public officials with an entity receiving $2.65 billion in federal funding — creating structural conditions for captured oversight.

### 8. MEDIUM — Adoption Metrics Mismatch
"Enrolled" was used by Infoway to describe program reach (16,185 prescribers, 7,953 pharmacies) when actual usage was below 5% of prescriptions. The 2009 OAG found Infoway had previously misrepresented physician access figures (claiming 38% when the actual figure was 22%). A pattern of misleading adoption metrics appears across Infoway's history.

### 9. STRUCTURAL — Arm's-Length Status as Accountability Shield
Infoway's status as an independent not-for-profit allows the federal government to argue it doesn't directly control Infoway's operations or contracts, while Parliament cannot compel Infoway directly (only Health Canada). This structure has consistently shielded $2.65+ billion in spending from meaningful parliamentary review.
