- [ ] Verify Metro Vancouver land area (~2,878 km² is the commonly cited

- [ ] Verify BC 2025 average farmland value ($10,477/acre or similar,
      likely FCC Farm Credit Canada farmland values report) and the implied
      $3.5B calculation.
- [ ] Trace the source of the Songhees Nation map image (1b) — likely a
      BC Ministry of Indigenous Relations and Reconciliation consultation
      document, a Te'mexw Treaty Association public notice, or a local
      news report (Times Colonist / CHEK). Needed to cite properly and to
      check whether it's the Te'mexw table's actual 1,916.7 ha or a
      broader illustrative package (existing reserve + fee-simple + Crown
      land options).
- [x] Find the actual legal status of "public access" on land already
      transferred under existing BC treaties (Tsawwassen 2009, Maa-nulth
      2011, Tla'amin 2016, Nisga'a 2000, Yale 2013) — **RESOLVED, see
      `docs/research-access-context.md` section 1.** Short answer: mixed.
      Default status is ordinary fee-simple private land (same public
      access rules as any private landowner). But every treaty examined
      also has documented, named examples that cut both ways: explicit
      recreational-access guarantees (Tla'amin, Yale, Nisga'a Public
      Lands), a protected-area *gain* (Maa-nulth's Thunderbird's Nest), a
      real named access restriction (Tla'amin designated all of Ahgykson/
      Harwood Island "Private Lands" with restricted access, disclosed in
      the treaty itself), and a real road-closure power (Nisga'a Final
      Agreement para 60(1) lets the Nation close certain non-village
      roads to the public). Not a "no access zone," but not a guarantee of
      blanket openness either — a negotiated, disclosed balance.
- [x] Get a First Nations / BC Treaty Commission / Te'mexw perspective on
      what these land transfers represent — **RESOLVED, see
      `docs/research-access-context.md` section 2.** BC Treaty Commission:
      treaties exchange defined, certain rights for legal certainty;
      settlement land is typically a small % of traditional territory
      (e.g. Tla'amin's 8,322 ha = ~2.6% of its 609,000 ha territory).
      Modern BC treaties (post-Nisga'a) explicitly do NOT use historic
      "cede, release and surrender" extinguishment language — Nisga'a uses
      a "modification" model instead. Also flagged: *Cowichan Tribes v.
      Canada*, 2025 BCSC 1490 (Aug 2025) is a separate, important, but
      distinct issue — Aboriginal title found over fee-simple land in
      Richmond that was NEVER part of a treaty — do not conflate with
      treaty settlement land.
- [ ] Check whether Hatley Castle/Royal Roads (federal land, per the map)
      is actually part of any current treaty table, since Note 28 only
      covers *provincial* Crown land commitments — reconcile the tweet's
      claim that includes it. **Still open** — research-access-context.md
      section 5 confirms Hatley Castle/Hatley Park IS one of 30 Crown
      parcels reportedly going to Te'mexw per Aug 2026 news coverage
      (conservativebc.ca, Epoch Times), but the federal-vs-provincial
      jurisdiction reconciliation itself was not resolved in this pass.
- [x] Quantify current reserve land totals in BC — **RESOLVED (with
      caveat), see `docs/research-access-context.md` section 3.** Best
      available figure: ~473,485 ha (4,734.85 km², ~0.4% of BC) per the
      *Encyclopedia of BC* (KnowBC) — no primary ISC/StatCan province-wide
      rollup figure could be located; ISC publishes per-band, not
      per-province, totals. That figure is ~3.5x the 135,425 ha pending
      commitment. Flag the secondary-source caveat in the report.

## 2a. Synthesis — verified facts and corrections vs. the original tweet

Full detail in `docs/research-primary-source.md`, `docs/research-benchmarks.md`,
`docs/research-songhees-victoria.md`, `docs/research-access-context.md`.

**Holds up:**
- 135,425 ha transcription from Note 28 — verified word-for-word against the
  official PDF.
- "47% of Metro Vancouver" — verified (47.0-47.2% depending on source).
- "~$3.5 billion" bottom line — verified, though the $/acre figure needed a
  small correction (see below).
- Federal-money / provincial-land split — confirmed verbatim across 5 years
  of Public Accounts.

**Needs correction or added context:**
- **Not a monotonic "committed and rising" trend.** 5-year data:
  2021/22 129,397 ha → 2022/23 132,980 → 2023/24 138,591 (peak) →
  2024/25 137,591 → 2025/26 135,425 ha (current). Down ~2.3% from peak.
- **Undercounts by ~1,179 ha (Yale).** Note 28 also discloses Yale First
  Nation's not-yet-effective 1,179 ha commitment separately; full note
  total is ~136,604 ha. (Yale's own 2010 fact sheet says 1,966 ha — an
  unresolved internal discrepancy, flag don't resolve.)
- **Farmland $/acre figure: correct to $10,577/acre (StatCan Table
  32-10-0047-01, 2025)**, not $10,477. Recomputed total still rounds to
  ~$3.5B. Farmland value is flagged as a weak proxy — the land is mostly
  non-agricultural Crown land, and BC farmland values themselves range
  43x by region (FCC 2025 report: $2,400-$104,600/acre).
- **135,425 ha is the *pending* figure, not the whole story.** Land
  ALREADY transferred under BC's 5 completed, in-effect modern treaties
  (Nisga'a, Tsawwassen, Maa-nulth, Tla'amin) totals ~232,746 ha — about
  1.7x larger than what's currently pending.
- **Hatley Castle is NOT part of the 135,425 ha or even the 1,916.7 ha
  Te'mexw figure** — it's federal DND land, outside Note 28's provincial
  scope entirely. The tweet conflates two different documents: the
  Public Accounts (provincial) and the Ministry of Indigenous Relations'
  "TTA Treaty Area and Treaty Lands" map (June 2026, provincial+federal).
  Confirmed as the real source of the map image, not a Songhees or
  advocacy document.
- Of what's shown in the Victoria map, only a few small parcels (Esquimalt
  113, Enterprise Wharf, Q Lot, a strip of Discovery Island park) are
  actual new provincial commitments. Hatley Park (federal, contested by a
  separate Esquimalt Nation legal claim filed Mar 27 2026) and "New
  Songhees 1A" (existing reserve since 1911) are not.
- **"No public access" — real but overstated as a blanket claim.** All 5
  effective BC treaties default settlement land to ordinary fee-simple
  private-land rules, AND all 5 contain explicit public-access-preserving
  clauses beyond that default. But real, named, treaty-text-documented
  narrowing exists too: Tla'amin designated all of Ahgykson/Harwood
  Island as private land with restricted access on the treaty's own
  effective date; the Nisga'a treaty lets the Nation close certain
  non-village roads. The live Te'mexw dispute itself has dueling public
  claims (BC Conservative Party says "no legal obligation" to keep parks
  open; the province says access "would still be safeguarded") — neither
  independently confirmed, since the actual Te'mexw agreement text isn't
  public yet.
- **Reserve land context:** BC's existing reserve land (~473,485 ha, best
  available secondary figure) is ~3.5x the pending 135,425 ha commitment
  — useful scale context, not part of either number.
- Treaty settlement land = fee simple, registered like any private BC
  land; modern treaties explicitly reject historic "extinguishment"
  language in favor of a "modification"/defined-rights model.
