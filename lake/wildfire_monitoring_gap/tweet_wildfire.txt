THREAD: France just announced 420 arrests for wildfire arson this season. Canada has arrested zero — because nobody's counting. So I pulled BC's own fire records back to 1950 to find the real story. What I found is stranger than an arson epidemic. 🧵

Dashboard: https://plots.canada-central.com/reports/wildfire-monitoring-gap.html

───────────────────────────────────────

1/ THE TRIGGER

France's Interior Ministry announced 420 arrests in 2026, including 166 minors, for deliberately or accidentally starting wildfires. Up from 48 in 2022.

It's a rolling, published tally. France counts this every season.

Does Canada have anything like it? I went looking.

───────────────────────────────────────

2/ THE ANSWER: NO

No Canadian agency — federal, provincial, or RCMP — publishes an annual count of wildfire-arson arrests.

Alberta RCMP said it plainly in June 2025: they weren't aware of ANY arson charges connected to that season's wildfires.

Not because nothing happened. Because nobody was centrally counting.

───────────────────────────────────────

3/ SO I WENT TO THE SOURCE

BC Wildfire Service keeps unusually good records — every fire since 1950, tagged Person, Lightning, or Unknown. Over 200,000 incidents.

I pulled all of it directly from BC's live data catalogue (not a news summary, the actual government dataset) and built the full history myself.

───────────────────────────────────────

4/ WHAT I FOUND WASN'T ARSON. IT WAS A CLIFF.

The share of BC wildfires with NO recorded cause was stable at 4-14% every single year from at least 1990 through 1998.

Then in one year:

  1998: 7.1% unknown
  1999: 38.2% unknown
  2000: 50.8% unknown

That's not a trend. That's a cliff.

───────────────────────────────────────

5/ AND IT NEVER WENT BACK

The unknown-cause rate stayed elevated — 40% to 78% depending on the year — for the next TWENTY YEARS. It peaked at 78.1% in 2011.

It's only partially recovered recently, sitting around 29% in the 2020s.

For two decades, BC couldn't tell you how most of its "known-cause" gap fires started.

───────────────────────────────────────

6/ I WENT LOOKING FOR WHY — HERE'S WHAT'S CONFIRMED

BC's forest service lost ~1,006 positions — roughly 25% of its workforce — in under a decade starting in the early 2000s. Field inspections by compliance/enforcement staff fell 46% between 2001/02 and 2004/05.
(Sierra Club BC / CCPA, cited in The Narwhal, 2024)

That explains why the gap PERSISTED.

───────────────────────────────────────

7/ BUT IT DOESN'T EXPLAIN WHY IT STARTED

Those cuts begin in 2001/02 — two to three years AFTER the jump already happened in 1999-2000.

I found a lead (BC's per-season incident data may trace to a 1999 system change) but couldn't verify it — the primary sources (Forest Service Protection Branch annual reports, 1998/99-2000/01) aren't accessible on the public web anymore.

I'm flagging this as UNCONFIRMED, not burying it.

───────────────────────────────────────

8/ WHAT WOULD CLOSE THE GAP

A freedom-of-information request to BC Wildfire Service for those 1998/99-2000/01 annual reports, plus data-dictionary documentation for the historical incident dataset.

That's a real, specific ask — not a vague "more research needed."

───────────────────────────────────────

9/ MEANWHILE, IN 2026, THE CUTS KEEP COMING

Government Operations Centre (Canada's 24/7 emergency-coordination centre): ~60 of ~110-130 staff got workforce-adjustment letters in January 2026.

"Gutting it by almost half."
— Sharon DeSousa, PSAC National President, July 2026

The government disputes it — says only 10 people, core staff 110→100.

Same cut. Different math.

───────────────────────────────────────

10/ AND NRCAN'S SATELLITE MAPPING TEAM

At least 17 remote-sensing staff — the people who produce Canada's satellite-based wildfire mapping — got workforce-adjustment notices in the same round.

"Raise questions about Canada's future wildfire-mapping capacity."
— PIPSC VP Stéphanie Fréchette, The Hill Times, July 2026

───────────────────────────────────────

11/ AND ECCC'S WEATHER MONITORING

Environment and Climate Change Canada disbanded its weather radar research team and ended the 230-transmitter Weatheradio network in March 2026.

Both feed fire-weather risk prediction.

All three cuts trace to the same $60B Comprehensive Expenditure Review — landing during one of the most intense wildfire seasons in Canadian history.

───────────────────────────────────────

12/ SO I ASKED A BIGGER QUESTION: DID THE PLANES KEEP UP?

If this has been a growing problem since 1999, did Canada's water bomber fleet and funding grow with it?

The honest answer surprised me. It's not simple negligence — but it's not adequate either.

───────────────────────────────────────

13/ BRITISH COLUMBIA — GROUND ZERO FOR WILDFIRES — OWNS ZERO WATER BOMBERS

Not one CL-215 or CL-415. BC relies entirely on 27 contracted aircraft.

Quebec has 14. Manitoba's fleet averages ~40 years old. Alberta's are 1986-88 builds. Newfoundland had a CL-415 sitting out of service since 2018.

National fleet estimates range 55-106 aircraft depending who's counting — there's no single clean number.

───────────────────────────────────────

14/ HERE'S THE PART THAT SURPRISED ME: IT'S NOT MOSTLY A MONEY PROBLEM

CL-415 production stopped in 2015. For NINE YEARS, zero new large water-scooper aircraft were built ANYWHERE IN THE WORLD.

Production only restarted in 2024 — after Europe (not Canada) placed a 22-aircraft order and forced De Havilland to rebuild a 50,000+-part supply chain from scratch.

───────────────────────────────────────

15/ EVEN WITH MONEY TODAY, YOU CAN'T BUY YOUR WAY OUT

Alberta ordered 5 new water bombers in February 2026 for $400 million.

First delivery: spring 2031. A five-year wait, no matter how fast the cheque clears.

Canada is capacity-constrained by a global manufacturing bottleneck it didn't cause and can't buy its way around.

───────────────────────────────────────

16/ WHAT THE FEDERAL MONEY ACTUALLY BUYS

Dec 2025: $257.6M over 4 years announced.
Feb 2026: revised to $316.7M over 5 years, redirected through CIFFC.
(Nobody has clarified if that's the same money restated or new money.)

May 2026: 10 aircraft LEASED for 150 days from a private operator.

That's short-term lease access to EXISTING aircraft. Not new fleet capacity.

───────────────────────────────────────

17/ "NOT YET A STRATEGY"

"Absolutely insufficient." — John Gradek, McGill aviation analyst, on Canada's ~55-aircraft fleet, 2023

"Not yet a strategy." — same analyst, on the May 2026 lease deal

"A pattern of underbudgeting... a clear lack of national coordination."
— James Moore, former federal minister, August 2026

───────────────────────────────────────

18/ AND ONTARIO CUT ITS OWN BUDGET, MID-CRISIS

Ontario's emergency forestry firefighting budget: $271M (2025-26) → $150M (2026-27).

Ontario burned through nearly DOUBLE the new budget's size the year before.

Its own 2022 Auditor General found wildland fire response times often exceeded four hours.

───────────────────────────────────────

19/ ONE MORE THING — I'M NOT CLAIMING AN ARSON EPIDEMIC

PolitiFact already fact-checked a viral "Canada wildfire arson epidemic" claim in 2023 and rated it FALSE. Most cited examples weren't actually 2023 wildfire arson.

I built this to include that guardrail on purpose. The real story here is a tracking and capacity gap — not a crime wave.

───────────────────────────────────────

20/ THE FULL PICTURE

  Unknown-cause fires: 7% → 38% in one year (1999), stayed high for 20 years
  0 Canadian agencies track wildfire-arson arrests annually, vs. France's 420
  BC: 0 water bombers of its own
  9 years — zero new large scoopers built worldwide (2015-2024)
  $60B Expenditure Review cutting emergency coordination mid-crisis
  Ontario cut its own firefighting budget nearly in half for 2026-27

None of these numbers are estimates. Every one is sourced, and every gap in the evidence is labeled as a gap, not smoothed over.
