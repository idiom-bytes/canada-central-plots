TWEET 1 (hook)
Canada's trucking industry faces a 2027 supply freeze — not from tariffs or trade wars.

Because Ottawa won't update three letters in a regulation.

🧵

---

TWEET 2 (the problem)
The US moved heavy-duty truck emissions certifications from EPA → NHTSA.

Canada's regs still only accept EPA certs.

US manufacturers no longer issue EPA certs for these trucks.

So Canadian dealers literally cannot import new American trucks.

~20,000 Class 8 trucks/year. 95% of our supply.

---

TWEET 3 (the absurdity)
The trucks meet every standard.
Safety: aligned.
Emissions: aligned.
Technology: identical.

The only problem is the acronym on the approval letter.

EPA ≠ NHTSA.

That's it. That's the whole crisis.

---

TWEET 4 (the timeline)
Manufacturers flagged this to Ottawa over a YEAR ago.

The real deadline isn't 2027.

It's August 2026 — when Canadian fleets place annual renewal orders.

No regulatory fix before August = no trucks ordered = no fleet renewal in 2027.

---

TWEET 5 (the sectors)
What runs on heavy trucks?

Shipping. Construction. Infrastructure. Forestry. Mining. Agriculture.

A frozen fleet means higher costs in every one of those sectors — and that lands on Canadian households.

All because of a missing reg update.

---

TWEET 6 (recommendation)
The fix:
Transport Canada amends the Motor Vehicle Safety Act regs to recognize NHTSA certs.

One amendment. Known fix. No policy debate required.

Ottawa: change the acronym. Save the supply chain.

The deadline is August. Not 2027. Now.
