# Trucking Certification Gap — Financial Impact Research
# Compiled: May 2026

## Annual Truck Volume (Canada)
- 2019 peak: ~23,300 Class 8 heavy-duty trucks
- 2025 (soft market, down 11%): ~17,000–20,000 units/year
- Canada ~7–8% of combined North America market (232,741 units in 2025)
- North America 2024: ~330,168 units → Canada ~23,000–26,000 at 7–8%
- Working estimate for 2026/2027 ordering cycle: 17,000–20,000 units

## Pricing
- Average new Class 8 truck (2020–2024): ~$195,000 USD
- At 1.36 CAD/USD: ~$265,000 CAD
- 2027-spec trucks carry an additional $20,000–$30,000 USD premium (~$27,000–$41,000 CAD)
  for new emissions-compliant powertrains

## Industry Context
- Canadian trucking industry annual revenue: $65B CAD
- Industry aftermarket (parts, service): $6.34B CAD (2025), forecast $7B (2026)
- Direct employment: 324,000 drivers + 180,000 in related roles
- % of Canada's international merchandise trade moved by road: 46%
- Industry contribution to GDP: ~3.76%

## Impact Segmentation

### 1. Truck Dealers & Manufacturers (~$4.5–5.3B direct)
- Canadian truck dealers: zero revenue from Class 8 new sales for 2027 model year
- U.S. manufacturers: lose Canadian order book (~17,000–20,000 units × $195K USD)
- Ripple: financing/leasing companies lose origination volume

### 2. Trucking Fleets / Operators
- Cannot renew aging fleets → higher per-mile operating costs
- Class 8 fleet contracted 0.7% in 2025; further contraction expected
- Average truck age rises → more maintenance downtime
- Fuel efficiency degrades: older trucks burn 5–10% more diesel than new
- On a fleet of 17,000+ un-renewed trucks, at ~100,000 km/year and ~$0.15/km fuel cost
  delta: ~$255M/year in excess fuel costs (rough estimate)
- Maintenance cost increase: industry estimates $15,000–$25,000/year more per aging truck
  → 17,000 trucks × $20,000 = ~$340M/year in incremental maintenance

### 3. Freight Rates / Logistics
- Capacity tightens as fleets can't expand or replace
- Freight rate increases historically follow fleet contraction
- A 5–10% capacity squeeze in a $65B revenue industry = $3.25–6.5B in rate pressure
  passed through to shippers

### 4. Downstream Sector Cost Pass-Through
Sectors that absorb higher freight costs:
- Shipping/Freight: direct (see above)
- Construction: materials transport, equipment delivery
- Infrastructure: road/utilities projects, public works
- Forestry: log/pulp haul
- Mining: ore/mineral haulage, often in time-sensitive cycles
- Agriculture: grain/livestock in compressed seasonal windows

### 5. Consumer / Business End-Point
- Higher freight rates → higher input costs → higher consumer prices
- Concentrated in goods-heavy sectors (food, fuel, building materials)
- No easy substitute: rail covers ~30% of freight ton-km but can't replicate HD truck flexibility

## Timeline of the Regulatory Gap

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| May 2018 | Canada harmonizes HD GHG regs with U.S. EPA Phase 2. Language: "EPA-certified engines may be sold in Canada." |
| April 2024 | Biden EPA finalizes Phase 3 GHG standards (MY2027+). EPA-only rule — NHTSA not co-author. Canada does not update. |
| March 12, 2025 | Trump EPA announces intent to rescind 2009 Endangerment Finding. Manufacturers immediately flag Ottawa: EPA certification will cease to exist. |
| ~May 2025 | Canadian manufacturers formally flag Transport Canada. No action taken. |
| February 12, 2026 | Trump EPA finalizes repeal. EPA's authority to certify GHG emissions eliminated. NHTSA becomes sole federal certifier. Gap is now a hard legal fact. |
| May 2026 | Canadian Truck Dealers Association goes public. Ottawa: "working on it." |
| August 2026 | Fleet ordering window for 2027 model year. Regulatory fix required by this date. |

Gap formally created: Feb 12, 2026 (~3 months before public disclosure)
Ottawa first warned: ~March–May 2025 (~14 months before ordering deadline)

## The Root Cause Layer
Canada's 2018 regulations were written assuming EPA would continue issuing GHG certifications
indefinitely. They contained no contingency for the certifying body ceasing to exist or being
replaced. Trump's deregulation eliminated EPA's legal authority to certify vehicle GHG emissions,
leaving NHTSA as the only remaining certifier. Canada has no mechanism to recognize NHTSA certs.
