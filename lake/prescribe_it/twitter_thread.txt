THREAD: The government spent $250 million on a prescription app that barely anyone used. Then Liberals blocked Parliament from seeing the contract. Here's what we know. 🧵

───────────────────────────────────────

1/ THE SETUP

Canada Health Infoway — a federal arm's-length corporation — was given $300 million in 2017 to build PrescribeIT: a digital system to replace faxed prescriptions.

The tech was built by TELUS Health on TELUS's own proprietary platform.

After 9 years: fewer than 5% of Canadian prescriptions were ever sent through it.

───────────────────────────────────────

2/ THE NUMBERS

To put that in perspective:

  Denmark:  >90% e-prescribing adoption
  USA:      >90% e-prescribing adoption
  Germany:  mandatory national system

  Canada (PrescribeIT): <5% after $250 million and 9 years.

On February 12, 2026, Infoway announced it was shutting the whole thing down. Effective May 29.

13/ THIS ISN'T NEW

This isn't PrescribeIT's first failure. It's Infoway's pattern.

The 2009 Auditor General found:

  - Infoway missed all 8 of its own program targets
  - 13 of 35 sampled contracts signed AFTER work began
  - One contract ballooned from $144K to $726K with no competitive bid
  - Infoway told Parliament 38% of doctors would have EMR access to secure $500M
    The real number was 22%

───────────────────────────────────────

14/ AND THEN THEY GOT MORE MONEY

A 2011 audit commissioned by Infoway's OWN board confirmed:

  - Missed self-established targets in all 8 major program areas
  - Only 16% of $2.168 billion was spent on actually helping clinicians adopt the systems

Then the federal government gave them $300 million more for PrescribeIT.

───────────────────────────────────────

15/ ZERO OVERSIGHT

No Auditor General has reviewed Canada Health Infoway since 2009/2011.

PrescribeIT ran its entire lifecycle — 9 years, $250 million — without a single independent OAG audit.

Total federal funding to Infoway since 2001: over $2.65 billion.

───────────────────────────────────────

16/ THE FEE THAT KILLED IT

In January 2025, Infoway started charging pharmacies $0.20 per prescription.

The Canadian Pharmacists Association surveyed 1,300+ pharmacists:
65% said they would quit PrescribeIT if the fee was implemented.

Infoway implemented it anyway.

The program collapsed shortly after.

17/ THE STRUCTURAL PROBLEM

PrescribeIT is not covered by Treasury Board procurement rules.

Infoway — as an arm's-length not-for-profit — runs its own procurement.

Parliament cannot compel Infoway directly. The government says it doesn't control Infoway's contracts.

$250 million. Zero Treasury Board oversight. Zero ATIP access to the contract.
