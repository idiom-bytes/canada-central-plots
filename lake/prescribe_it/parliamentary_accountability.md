# PrescribeIT Parliamentary Accountability: Research Report

**Research Date:** April 14, 2026  
**Scope:** Parliamentary accountability, media criticism, and political controversy surrounding PrescribeIT and Canada Health Infoway's $250 million federal investment

---

## 1. Program Overview and Shutdown

PrescribeIT, Canada's national e-prescribing service operated by Canada Health Infoway, was launched in 2017 after Infoway selected TELUS Health as the technical solution provider. After spending over $250 million in federal funds, Canada Health Infoway announced on **February 12, 2026** that PrescribeIT would cease operations on **May 29, 2026**, citing low adoption rates and the absence of a sustainable cost-sharing model with provinces and territories.

- Total program cost: **over $250 million** since 2017 ($35 million/year in recent years)
- Key failure metric: **fewer than 5% of prescriptions** in Canada sent electronically through PrescribeIT
- The program attempted a private-sector takeover process (managed by PwC) before the board moved to cancel entirely

## 2. HESA Parliamentary Committee Activity (45th Parliament)

### 2.1 Conservative Document Motion — March 12, 2026 (HESA Meeting 25)

Conservative MP **Matt Strauss** (Kitchener South–Hespeler) moved a sweeping motion at the House of Commons Standing Committee on Health (HESA) ordering production of unredacted government documents. The motion, debated across two committee meetings (March 12 and March 24, 2026), specifically requested the following PrescribeIT materials:

> "all documents related to PrescribeIT, including: the 2017 contract between Canada Health Infoway and TELUS Health; all amendments, renewals, and change orders since 2017; all intellectual property ownership agreements; the 2026 termination notice and all clauses, provisions, or records relating to termination and penalties; a list of all payments made by Canada Health Infoway to TELUS Health since 2017"

The motion also requested ministerial recommendation documents and set a deadline of **April 17, 2026** for production, with reporting to the House by April 20.

Conservative MP **Burton Bailey** (Red Deer) separately asked the deputy minister to table "the department's findings and analysis on PrescribeIT."

**Source:** [HESA Meeting 25 Minutes, Parliament of Canada](https://www.ourcommons.ca/documentviewer/en/45-1/HESA/meeting-25/minutes)

### 2.2 Liberal Amendment to Block PrescribeIT Disclosure — March 24, 2026 (HESA Meeting 25)

At the March 24, 2026 continuation of HESA Meeting 25, Liberal MP **Sonia Sidhu** (Brampton South) moved an amendment to **delete the PrescribeIT-specific content** from the document production motion. Sidhu's amendment proposed removing:

> "the 2017 contract between Canada Health Infoway and TELUS Health; all amendments, renewals, and change orders since 2017; all intellectual property ownership agreements; the 2026 termination notice and all clauses, provisions, or records relating to termination and penalties; a list of all payments made by Canada Health Infoway to TELUS Health since 2017"

The amendment would have retained only the ministerial recommendation documents while stripping out the contract terms, IP agreements, termination clauses, and full payment history — the most accountability-sensitive materials.

Before a vote on Sidhu's amendment could occur, Bloc Québécois MP **Maxime Blanchette-Joncas** moved to adjourn debate. That procedural motion passed 5-0, ending the March 24 meeting without resolution. The original document motion remained unresolved.

**Source:** [HESA Meeting 25 Minutes, Parliament of Canada](https://www.ourcommons.ca/documentviewer/en/45-1/HESA/meeting-25/minutes)

**Note on the "filibuster" framing:** The specific phrase "Chief Filibuster Officer" or a 2-hour single-MP filibuster was not identified in verifiable parliamentary records. What was documented is procedurally equivalent: a Liberal amendment designed to excise the PrescribeIT contract disclosures, followed by a procedural adjournment that prevented a vote. This is a recognized parliamentary delay tactic. OpenParliament.ca's search results confirm Matt Strauss said at committee: "The Liberals blew $250 million on PrescribeIT. That is the same amount they spent on the sponsorship scandal."

### 2.3 Standing Order 106(4) Meeting Scheduled

A subsequent meeting was listed on OpenParliament.ca as **Health Committee Meeting 27 (April 15, 2026)** with agenda item: *"Meeting Requested Pursuant to Standing Order 106(4) to Discuss the March 12, 2026 Production of Document Motion."* This indicates that Conservative MPs triggered a special meeting to revisit the unresolved PrescribeIT document production order. As of April 14, 2026, this meeting had not yet occurred and transcripts were unavailable.

**Source:** [OpenParliament.ca Health Committee Meeting 27](https://openparliament.ca/committees/health/45-1/27/)

### 2.4 HESA March 24, 2026 Hearing — Minister and Deputy Minister Testimony

At HESA Meeting 25 (March 24 continuation), **Shalene Curtis-Micallef** (Deputy Minister, Health Canada) described PrescribeIT as "an initiative that was started by Canada Health Infoway," while **Sonia Sidhu** (Liberal) characterized it as "a national e-prescribing service that is helping doctors and pharmacists communicate better to reduce errors" — remarkable framing for a program that had already been cancelled six weeks earlier.

## 4. Adoption Statistics vs. Stated Targets

### 4.1 Official Metrics at Time of Cancellation

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Prescriptions sent via PrescribeIT | **< 5%** of all Canadian prescriptions |
| Prescribers enrolled (end of 2023) | **16,185** |
| Pharmacies enrolled (end of 2023) | **7,953** |
| Cumulative transactions (end of 2023) | **79.8 million** |
| Annual program cost (recent years) | **~$35 million/year** |
| Total cost since 2017 | **> $250 million** |

Sources: Globe and Mail; [PrescribeIT 2023 Year in Review](https://www.prescribeit.ca/2023-year-in-review); [PrescribeIT 2024 Year in Review](https://www.prescribeit.ca/2024-year-in-review)

### 4.2 The Enrollment vs. Usage Gap

This is the core accountability problem: thousands of pharmacies and doctors enrolled, yet fewer than 5% of prescriptions were actually transmitted through the service. A 2023 System and Use Survey showed 80% of prescribers and 70% of pharmacists *favored* e-prescribing for efficiency — yet they still weren't using PrescribeIT. The program suffered from a structural misalignment: healthcare providers bore the costs while the benefits accrued to the broader system.

Expert analysis (Dr. Karim Keshavjee, via [Days of Past Futures blog post](https://prich5757.wordpress.com/2026/02/23/prescribing-for-failure-the-canadian-e-prescribing-experience/)):

> "The simplified reason for the failure of PrescribeIT" was that participating physicians and pharmacists "had to bear the costs for implementing and maintaining" while "society...saw the benefits." Provider incentives will drive adoption, not just data exchange capabilities.

By contrast, Denmark and the United States achieved **>90% e-prescribing adoption rates** through mandates, financial incentives, and penalties for non-use.

### 4.3 The 2025 Fee Fiasco

In 2025, as federal funding declined, Canada Health Infoway began charging pharmacies **$0.20 per prescription** to use PrescribeIT. This directly accelerated program failure:

- A **2024 Canadian Pharmacists Association survey** of 1,300+ pharmacists found that **65% would abandon PrescribeIT** if the fee was implemented
- CPhA CEO Glen Doucet stated: "Pharmacies are being asked to absorb the cost of a solution that benefits the entire health-care system."
- CPhA escalated the issue directly to the federal Minister of Health

**Sources:**
- [CPhA Press Release, December 17, 2024](https://www.pharmacists.ca/news-events/news/cpha-urges-pause-on-prescribeit-transaction-fee-that-will-threaten-the-sustainability-of-canadian-pharmacies/)
- [Canadian Healthcare Technology: Infoway pulls the plug](https://www.canhealth.com/2026/02/18/infoway-pulls-the-plug-on-prescribeit/)

## 5. Government Response and Accountability Gap

### 5.1 Minister and Spokesperson Statements

Health Minister **Marjorie Michel**'s spokesperson Guillaume Bertrand stated on cancellation:

> "Since its introduction, and despite the extensive work of our partner Infoway, the adoption remains below anticipated levels."

Bertrand cited "the lack of a federal-provincial-territorial cost-sharing model" as the ultimate cause — a structural problem that had existed since the program's inception and was not flagged publicly until cancellation.

The Carney government has not convened dedicated parliamentary hearings into the $250 million expenditure. Health Minister Michel tabled the 2024-2025 Canada Health Act Annual Report on February 13, 2026 — the day after the PrescribeIT cancellation announcement — without a dedicated parliamentary statement on PrescribeIT.

## 6. The TELUS Health Contract and IP Controversy

### 6.1 Contract Details

Canada Health Infoway selected TELUS Health as the sole technical solution provider for PrescribeIT in **May 2017**, building the platform on TELUS Health's "TELUS Health Exchange" platform. Key accountability concerns:

- TELUS Health **owns much of the intellectual property** associated with PrescribeIT
- TELUS collected **millions of dollars in licensing and service fees** annually since 2017
- The 2026 termination notice, termination penalties, and payment records since 2017 are **precisely the documents Conservatives sought** and Liberals sought to exclude

**Sources:**
- [Newswire: "Infoway Selects TELUS Health for PrescribeIT™" (May 11, 2017)](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/infoway-selects-telus-health-for-prescribeit-621970253.html)
- Globe and Mail reporting on IP ownership

### 6.2 What Was Not Disclosed

Parliament has not received public disclosure of:
- The contract value paid to TELUS Health over 9 years
- The terms of IP ownership that left a private telecom owning publicly-funded health infrastructure
- Termination clause details and any penalties or wind-down costs
- What became of the intellectual property after shutdown

The Conservative document motion of March 12, 2026 was specifically designed to surface this information.

## 7. Canada Health Infoway's History of Accountability Problems

PrescribeIT's failure is not Infoway's first. The organization has received **$2.45 billion in federal funding** since its establishment in 2001, with a documented history of missed targets and weak oversight.

### 7.1 Auditor General Findings (2009)

Auditor General Sheila Fraser's 2009 report to Parliament found:

- Only **17% of doctors** had electronic medical records despite hundreds of millions spent
- Infoway missed its stated target of 28% by 11 percentage points
- Contracting problems: one contract ballooned to **$726,000 from $144,000** without competitive bids
- In a sample of 35 contracts, **13 were signed only after work had already begun** (retroactive contracting)
- The funding agreement was so poorly crafted that Infoway was not required to provide enough information to Health Canada to verify expenditure validity
- "The information in support of the initial instalment of the new funding was inadequate to verify the validity and appropriateness of the projected expenditures"

**Sources:**
- [CBC News: "Federal eHealth project has holes: auditor general"](https://www.cbc.ca/news/science/federal-ehealth-project-has-holes-auditor-general-1.825287)
- [CMAJ: "Audit concludes Infoway missed program targets"](https://www.cmaj.ca/content/183/8/893)

### 7.2 External Performance Audit (2011)

A 2011 external audit commissioned by Infoway's own board (Bell Browne Molnar & Delicate Consulting) found:

- Infoway **missed its self-established targets in all eight major program areas**
- Only **16% of the $2.168 billion** earmarked was devoted to helping clinicians actually acquire and use electronic health systems
- Adoption targets were set for only ~40% of spending where such targets were warranted

**Source:** [CMAJ: "Audit concludes Infoway missed program targets"](https://www.cmaj.ca/content/183/8/893)

### 7.3 Expert Calls for Independent Watchdog (2011)

In a 2011 CMAJ article, experts called for an independent oversight body for Canada's ehealth programs:

- **Dominic Covvey** (NIHI President): called explicitly for a "watch-dog" independent of government
- **Dr. Brian Postl** (CIHI Chair): noted "somebody is paying attention and making sure that we are progressing"
- Key finding: Infoway has **no mandate to independently evaluate its own performance**; internal evaluations constitute "government evaluating itself"
- Canada ranked **21st globally in health IT development** (WHO data), trailing Scandinavia, Europe, and Korea

**Source:** [PMC: "Experts call for health infoway 'watchdog'"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3042437/)

## 8. Healthcare Provider Criticism

### 8.1 Pharmacist Association Opposition

The Canadian Pharmacists Association (CPhA) was the most vocal professional organization:

- Filed formal opposition to the $0.20/prescription fee in December 2024
- Published survey showing 65% of pharmacists would quit the service
- Called for fee pause and a fair cost-sharing model
- Escalated concerns directly to the Health Minister
- CPhA noted the fee was being implemented mid-ownership-transition, creating additional risk

**Source:** [CPhA: "CPhA urges pause on PrescribeIT transaction fee"](https://www.pharmacists.ca/news-events/news/cpha-urges-pause-on-prescribeit-transaction-fee-that-will-threaten-the-sustainability-of-canadian-pharmacies/)

CPhA also submitted a pre-budget brief to the Finance Committee in 2025 calling for maintained federal funding to support PrescribeIT's transition to an alternative governance structure.

**Source:** [Canadian Pharmacists Association Pre-Budget Submission 2025 to FINA](https://www.ourcommons.ca/Content/Committee/441/FINA/Brief/BR13231868/br-external/CanadianPharmacistsAssociation-e.pdf)

### 8.2 Physicians — "Back to the Dark Ages"

Ontario family doctors expressed significant alarm at the abrupt shutdown:

- Family doctors **Colin Bolzon** and **Rebecca Robinson** said PrescribeIT was essential to their practice and they would be forced back to faxing
- One physician: "I feel like we're going back to the dark ages"
- Dr. James Owen (St. Michael's Hospital, Toronto): "It feels like we are already long overdue for health care to make the transition to secure electronic communication"
- Doctors flagged that reverting to fax increases prescription fraud risk

**Source:** [CBC News: "Back to the fax? Doctors say Ottawa's plans to axe prescription software leaves them in limbo"](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/london/prescribe-it-9.7127925)

### 8.3 Alberta College of Pharmacy

Notified members that TPP Type 1 monitored medications (controlled substances) would need to revert to **physical secure prescription pads** after May 29, 2026 — a direct patient safety regression.

**Source:** [Alberta College of Pharmacy: "PrescribeIT e-prescribing service to cease operations May 29, 2026"](https://abpharmacy.ca/news/prescribeit-e-prescribing-service-to-cease-operations-may-29-2026/)

## 10. What Parliament Has Not Received

As of April 14, 2026, the following accountability information has **not been publicly disclosed** despite Conservative document requests:

1. **The 2017 contract** between Canada Health Infoway and TELUS Health — including the original contract value, scope, and competitive process details
2. **All amendments and renewals** to the TELUS Health contract over 9 years
3. **Intellectual property ownership agreements** — who owns what, and what happens to the IP now that PrescribeIT is shut down
4. **The 2026 termination notice** — what termination clauses were triggered and whether taxpayers face penalties or clawback provisions
5. **Total payments to TELUS Health** from 2017 to 2026 — despite TELUS collecting "millions in licensing and service fees annually," the exact amounts are unknown
6. **Health Canada's internal analysis** of the program's failures and the decision to cancel

## 11. Structural Accountability Gaps

### 11.1 Infoway's Arm's-Length Status as a Shield

Canada Health Infoway is incorporated as an independent, not-for-profit corporation. This structure has historically been used to limit parliamentary accountability:
- Health Canada can argue it does not directly control Infoway's operations or contracts
- Infoway's board is made up of deputy ministers — unelected officials who do not testify to Parliament
- The funding agreement was critiqued by the Auditor General for inadequate information requirements

### 11.2 No Audit Since 2009/2011

Despite receiving hundreds of millions in additional federal funding post-2011 (including the entire PrescribeIT program from 2017–2026), no public Auditor General performance audit of Canada Health Infoway has been identified since the 2009 report. The PrescribeIT program ran its entire lifecycle without publicly available independent performance auditing.

### 11.3 Revolving Door — Limited Public Information

No documented cases of a specific revolving-door pattern between Canada Health Infoway leadership and TELUS Health were identified in publicly available sources as of April 2026. However, Infoway's CEO Michael Green has been in place since 2014 and the organization's team page does not publicly disclose prior employment history. The lobbying registry and executive backgrounds warrant further investigation.

**Source:** [Canada Health Infoway Team Page](https://www.infoway-inforoute.ca/en/about-us/our-team)

## 12. Comparators and Context

### 12.1 The Sponsorship Scandal Comparison

Conservative MP Matt Strauss explicitly compared PrescribeIT spending to the Liberal Sponsorship Scandal of the early 2000s — also approximately $250 million in misspent public funds — in his March 2026 committee statement.

**Source:** [OpenParliament.ca debate search: PrescribeIT](https://openparliament.ca/search/?q=prescribeit&type=debate)

### 12.2 International Comparators

- **Denmark:** >90% e-prescribing adoption through mandates and incentives
- **United States:** >90% e-prescribing adoption through financial incentives (HITECH Act) and EHR certification requirements
- **Germany:** Implemented mandatory e-prescription system nationally
- **Canada (PrescribeIT):** <5% adoption after 9 years and $250 million
