**Research date:** April 14, 2026

## 1. What Is PrescribeIT?

PrescribeIT is Canada's national electronic prescribing (e-prescribing) service, designed to allow authorized prescribers (physicians, nurse practitioners) to digitally transmit prescriptions directly to a patient's pharmacy of choice, replacing paper prescriptions and faxes. The system integrates into electronic medical records (EMR) systems and pharmacy management systems (PMS).

**Key stated features:**
- Secure, private, directed prescription transmission
- No dropped faxes, no illegible handwriting, no missed phone calls
- Provided as a no-cost service to prescribers (until 2025 when a fee was introduced for pharmacies)
- Opioid monitoring support
- Prescription fraud reduction

## 2. Who Created and Operates PrescribeIT?

### Canada Health Infoway (Operator)

Canada Health Infoway (Infoway) is an **independent, not-for-profit organization** created by Canada's First Ministers in **2001**, funded exclusively by the federal Government of Canada. It is structured as a federal Crown corporation operating at arm's length from government. Its corporate members are the **deputy ministers of health** from all 14 federal, provincial, and territorial jurisdictions.

**Total federal funding received (2001–2018):** $2.45 billion (as of 2018)

Infoway's mandate is to accelerate the adoption of digital health solutions across Canada, including electronic health records (EHR), e-prescribing, and virtual care.

**Federal funding history (PrescribeIT-relevant):**
- **2001:** $500 million seed funding (First Ministers' Action Plan)
- **2003:** $600 million (First Ministers' Health Accord)
- **2004:** $100 million (pandemic surveillance)
- **2007:** $400 million (EHR and wait times)
- **2010:** $500 million (EHR/EMR expansion)
- **2016:** $50 million over two years — specifically to support **e-prescribing and telehomecare**
- **2017:** $300 million over five years — specifically to **expand e-prescribing** (PrescribeIT), virtual care, and EMR adoption
- **2019:** $36.5 million (organ donation data system)
- **2020:** $50 million (virtual care COVID expansion)

### TELUS Health (Technical Developer)

TELUS Health — a division of TELUS Corp. — was selected as the **technical solution provider** for PrescribeIT via a competitive bidding process announced **May 11, 2017**.

PrescribeIT was built on the **TELUS Health Exchange** platform, described as "open, interoperable, and vendor-agnostic."

**Critical IP ownership issue:** After the shutdown announcement, it was confirmed that **TELUS Health retains ownership of much of the intellectual property** underlying PrescribeIT. Only a portion of the standard is Infoway's IP; the transmission hub standard is proprietary to TELUS. TELUS Health collected **millions of dollars in licensing and service fees each year** throughout the program's life.

## 4. Canada Health Infoway's Role

Infoway designed, operated, governed, and was accountable for PrescribeIT. The program was accountable to Infoway's Board of Directors and to its Corporation Members (the deputy ministers of health for federal, provincial, and territorial governments).

**Governance framework:**
- A dedicated **PrescribeIT Strategy and Industry Alliances Committee** existed within the Infoway Board
- Five stakeholder advisory groups: opioids working group, vendor partner forum, pharmacy advisory sub-committee, physician advisory sub-committee, clinician advisory sub-committee
- Infoway's funding agreement with Health Canada expired **March 31, 2026**

**What Infoway said at shutdown:**  
"PrescribeIT has not reached the scale required to support long-term sustainability" and "no viable model has emerged to support the continued operation of a single, centrally managed, national service." In 2024, Infoway issued a Request for Expression of Interest (RFEOI) seeking a joint venture partner, acquisition, or other structure — this process failed to produce a viable partner.

## 6. Health Canada's Involvement and Funding Decisions

Health Canada is the federal department responsible for funding Infoway through contribution agreements (grants and up-front multi-year funding). Infoway is not directly part of Health Canada — it operates at arm's length — but its budget depends entirely on federal transfers channelled through Health Canada.

The 2017 federal budget allocated **$300 million over five years** to Infoway specifically to expand e-prescribing (PrescribeIT) and virtual care. This was the primary federal funding envelope that seeded the $250M+ spent on PrescribeIT.

**Health Canada's funding agreement with Infoway expired March 31, 2026** — a key deadline that preceded the May 29 service shutdown. The absence of a renewed agreement was central to the shutdown rationale.

**Official Health Canada position at shutdown:**  
Spokesperson Guillaume Bertrand stated: "Since its introduction, and despite the extensive work of our partner Infoway, the adoption remains below anticipated levels." He also cited the "absence of a federal-provincial-territorial cost-sharing model" as the reason the service was discontinued.

**Supplementary information tables** in Health Canada's departmental reports confirm Infoway received "up-front multi-year funding" (grants rather than annual contributions), which explains why there is no single ProActive Disclosure contract record for PrescribeIT.

## 9. Auditor General Reports on Canada Health Infoway

### Fall 2009 Report — Chapter 4 (Auditor General Sheila Fraser)
Released **November 3, 2009**. This is the most significant OAG review of Infoway.

**Key findings:**
- Infoway received $1.6 billion in federal funding to build electronic health record systems
- Infoway showed "due regard" for taxpayer money in financial management (overall positive verdict)
- **Infoway missed its self-established targets in all eight major program areas**
- Adoption targets were set for only ~40% of spending where such targets were warranted
- The goal of EHR systems being "available" to 50% of Canadians by 2010 was potentially misleading — "available" did not mean "used" or "adopted"
- **38% vs. 22% physician access discrepancy:** When lobbying Parliament for an additional $500M in 2010, Infoway stated 38% of physicians would have access to EMRs by March 31, 2010. Once funded, the actual figure was revealed to be **22%** — 60% lower than claimed
- When the government allocated $500M in 2010 for clinician support, Infoway diverted **over 30%** to other projects
- Only 16% of the $2.168B allocated was earmarked to help clinicians acquire and use electronic health information systems

**OAG Recommendations:**
1. Infoway should report annually on actual adoption by health care professionals
2. Infoway should compare actual vs. expected results across five core systems
3. Health Canada should develop better monitoring frameworks for compliance with funding agreements
4. Infoway should reconsider its strategy "if projects are not completed, or adoption targets are not met"

**Follow-up independent evaluation (March 2018):** Concluded "Infoway has greatly contributed to more timely delivery of health care." (This evaluation was commissioned by Infoway, raising neutrality questions.)

## 10. Promised Outcomes vs. Actual Outcomes

### What Was Promised (2017)

| Promised Outcome | Source |
|---|---|
| Replace faxed prescriptions nationally with a secure digital system | Original program rationale |
| "Available to all community prescribers and community pharmacies across Canada" (ACCESS 2022 goal) | Infoway's ACCESS 2022 strategic plan |
| Trial runs in Alberta and Ontario by mid-2017; full national rollout by mid-2018 | May 2017 TELUS Health press release |
| Support opioid monitoring and reduce prescription fraud | Infoway/TELUS Health announcement |
| Become financially self-sustaining through federal-provincial-territorial cost sharing | Infoway governance documents |
| Enhance medication safety and patient privacy nationally | PrescribeIT program materials |

### What Was Delivered (2017–2026)

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| **Adoption rate** | Less than 5% of all Canadian prescriptions sent through PrescribeIT |
| **Provinces live** | 8 provinces/territories (with Quebec in trial) — never achieved full national rollout |
| **Prescribers and pharmacies** | "Thousands signed up" but few using it actively |
| **Cost-sharing model** | Never achieved — provinces/territories refused to share operational costs |
| **Financial sustainability** | Never achieved — entirely dependent on federal funding throughout; fee introduction in 2025 backfired |
| **TELUS Health IP** | TELUS retained ownership of most intellectual property despite $250M+ in public investment |
| **Private sector takeover** | RFEOI in 2024 found no viable partner |
| **Fax replacement** | Failed — majority of prescriptions still sent by fax or paper in 2026 |
| **Total cost** | $250M+ (appx. $35M/year in recent years) |

### Why It Failed — Expert Analysis

Dr. **Karim Keshavjee** (Program Director of Health Informatics, University of Toronto Institute of Health Policy, Management and Evaluation) presented at the FHLIP2026 conference in Toronto in February 2026. His core finding: **"Provider incentives will drive adoption, not just data exchange capabilities."**

Structural failure identified:
- Physicians bore implementation and maintenance costs
- Physicians received no direct financial advantage from using PrescribeIT
- The system was voluntary — unlike Denmark or the United States, which achieved >90% e-prescribing adoption through mandatory requirements, financial incentives, or penalties for non-compliance
- Canada employed none of these strategies

The $0.20 per-prescription fee introduced in January 2025 accelerated collapse: a CPhA survey of 1,300+ pharmacists found **65% would discontinue use if the fee was introduced**. The fee was implemented anyway, contributing to further adoption drops.

## 12. What Replaces PrescribeIT?

The federal government introduced **Bill S-5** (the *Connected Care for Canadians Act*) on **February 4, 2026**, in the Senate of the 45th Parliament. Health Minister Marjorie Michel sponsored the bill.

The bill is substantially similar to **Bill C-72** (introduced June 2024; died when Parliament was prorogued).

**What Bill S-5 does:**
- Requires all health information technology vendors to adopt common interoperability standards
- Prohibits "data blocking" practices
- Does not recreate a centralized government-run platform — instead mandates an open standard approach

**Open standard transition:**
- By **May 1, 2026:** Canada Health Infoway to publish the national ePrescribing open standard publicly
- From **May 30, 2026:** Infoway will maintain the open standard but will no longer operate any e-prescribing service

The open standard will allow any health technology company to build interoperable e-prescribing systems. Whether adoption will improve without mandates or incentives remains an open question given the failure analysis.

## 13. Public Opinion and Survey Data

From Infoway's own 2023 Canadian Digital Health Survey:
- 83% of Canadians interested in having a prescription sent directly to their pharmacy without a paper prescription
- 77% interested in electronic prescription renewal requests
- 73% support mandatory electronic prescribing of controlled substances such as opioids

Despite overwhelming public support, provider adoption remained below 5%.
