# PrescribeIT & Canada Health Infoway: Lobbying and Procurement Research Report

**Prepared:** April 14, 2026  
**Sources:** lobbycanada.gc.ca, open.canada.ca, canada.ca, infoway-inforoute.ca, Globe and Mail, CBC News, CMAJ, Canadian Healthcare Technology

---

## Executive Summary

Canada Health Infoway (Infoway) has received approximately **$2.65+ billion** in cumulative federal funding since 2001, operating as a federally incorporated not-for-profit that sits outside normal Treasury Board procurement rules. Its flagship e-prescribing program PrescribeIT, built on TELUS Health proprietary technology at a cost of over **$250 million** since 2017, was cancelled effective May 29, 2026 after achieving fewer than 5% prescription adoption. TELUS Health retains ownership of much of the program's intellectual property. No formal in-house lobbying registration for Canada Health Infoway itself appears in the federal Lobbyists Registration System, though Infoway employs senior government-relations staff and makes pre-budget submissions to Parliament. TELUS Health and its parent TELUS Corporation maintain active lobbying registrations with Health Canada and other departments.

## 1. Lobbying Registry (lobbycanada.gc.ca)

### 1.1 Canada Health Infoway: No Formal Registration Found

A search of the federal Lobbyists Registration System ([lobbycanada.gc.ca](https://lobbycanada.gc.ca/app/secure/ocl/lrs/do/advSrch)) returns **no in-house or consultant lobbying registration under the name "Canada Health Infoway" or "PrescribeIT"** in the searchable registry. A direct site search for "Infoway" also returns no results.

This absence does not necessarily mean Infoway has not engaged in government relations. Under the *Lobbying Act*, in-house lobbyists must register only when lobbying constitutes a "significant part" of their duties (generally interpreted as 20% or more of a single employee's time). Infoway's public-facing government relations appear structured to avoid triggering this threshold, while still engaging extensively with federal officials through contribution agreement governance mechanisms, parliamentary committee submissions, and senior staff meetings with Health Canada.

**Key finding:** Canada Health Infoway does not appear to be a registered lobbying entity under the federal *Lobbying Act*. However, the organization employs Angela Jonsson (Executive VP, Public Affairs, Corporate Strategy & Communications) and maintains active relationships with Health Canada officials. Infoway has also submitted formal pre-budget briefs directly to parliamentary committees.

## 5. PrescribeIT Procurement: The TELUS Health Contract

### 5.1 Procurement Process

In July 2016, Canada Health Infoway issued a **Call for Proposals** (a competitive RFP process) for a **$40 million national e-prescribing service** to be called PrescribeIT. The initial $40M came from Budget 2016.

**Key procurement facts:**
- Infoway invited bids for "an e-prescribing service that will provide for the secure electronic transmission of a prescription from a prescriber to the patient's pharmacy of choice"
- The selection process is described by Infoway as a competitive bid with TELUS Health named as "the successful bidder"
- The selection announcement explicitly states TELUS Health was chosen from a competitive process, though the number of competing bidders and selection criteria details were not publicly disclosed
- CMAJ reported the initial budget was $40M; Budget 2017 then added $300M over 5 years
- Source: [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5008949/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5008949/) (CMAJ coverage of the RFP)

**Important timing:** Charles Harnick registered as a consultant lobbyist for TELUS Health Solutions GP in September 2016, the same month the PrescribeIT RFP was being evaluated. His registration covered Government Procurement and Health Canada specifically.

### 5.2 Contract Award to TELUS Health

- **Awarded:** May 11, 2017
- **Selected by:** Canada Health Infoway (not the federal government directly)
- **Platform used:** TELUS Health Exchange, a proprietary TELUS platform
- **Initial trial:** Mid-2017 in Alberta and Ontario communities
- **Full rollout planned:** Mid-2018 across Canada
- **IP arrangement:** TELUS Health retains ownership of the proprietary transmission hub technology; Infoway holds some of the e-prescribing standard IP
- Source: [https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/infoway-selects-telus-health-for-prescribeit-621970253.html](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/infoway-selects-telus-health-for-prescribeit-621970253.html)

### 5.3 Contract Scale and Costs

- **TELUS Health was paid "millions of dollars in licensing and service fees each year"** from PrescribeIT revenues
- PrescribeIT's annual costs reached approximately **$35 million** in recent years
- Total program cost from 2017–2026: **over $250 million**
- A substantial but unspecified portion of this flowed to TELUS Health as licensing and service fees
- Infoway cited contractual obligations as the reason it could not comment on the shutdown — suggesting the wind-down involved complex contract negotiations with TELUS Health
- Source: [https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/economy/article-federal-axe-the-fax-e-prescribing-service-built-with-telus-health-to/](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/economy/article-federal-axe-the-fax-e-prescribing-service-built-with-telus-health-to/)

### 5.4 Intellectual Property Concern

The Globe and Mail reported that **Telus Health retains ownership of much of the intellectual property** associated with PrescribeIT, despite the program being built almost entirely with public funds. After spending $250+ million in public money, the government cannot simply hand the platform to another provider — the IP belongs to TELUS. This is a significant public accountability concern in a non-competitive or lightly-regulated procurement context.

### 5.5 Attempted Private Buyout

Before announcing cancellation, Infoway attempted to find **a private company to take over PrescribeIT** and continue operating it. This effort failed. The program was then cancelled effective May 29, 2026.

## 7. Auditor General Findings (2009–2010)

Auditor General Sheila Fraser's 2009 report on Canada Health Infoway found a **raft of contracting and reporting problems**:

### 7.1 Contract Amendment Abuse
- A $144,000 competitive contract was **amended five times**, raising its total value to **$726,000** — with no competition for the amended amounts
- Fraser stated: "This practice is not conducive to the fair and transparent awarding of contracts and it raises questions about the appropriateness of Infoway's contracting policy"
- Source: [https://www.theglobeandmail.com/news/politics/health-canada-slammed-in-ehealth-audit/article1208858/](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/news/politics/health-canada-slammed-in-ehealth-audit/article1208858/)

### 7.2 Work Commencing Before Contracts Signed
- In a sample of 35 contracts examined, **13 were signed only after work had already begun**
- Source: [https://www.cbc.ca/news/science/federal-ehealth-project-has-holes-auditor-general-1.825287](https://www.cbc.ca/news/science/federal-ehealth-project-has-holes-auditor-general-1.825287)

### 7.3 Health Canada Accountability Failures
- The auditors found the contribution agreement between Health Canada and Infoway was "so badly crafted that Infoway was not required to provide enough information to Health Canada to ensure the money would be well spent"
- An associate deputy minister **retroactively approved** a cheque that had already been cashed — 11 months after the payment was made
- Unauthorized travel expenses were flagged
- Internal compliance audits lacked "full objectivity and independence"
- Documentation for the initial $38.7-million payment was inadequate

### 7.4 Performance Reporting Failures
- Infoway claimed success in creating EHRs for 17% of Canadians — **without disclosing this was 11 percentage points short of its own target**
- Infoway missed its self-established targets in all eight major program areas
- Adoption targets had only been set for approximately 40% of spending where such targets were warranted

### 7.5 Unknown Total Costs
- A concurrent provincial audit found that **total costs of the EHR initiative were unknown** because provinces failed to consistently track expenses
- Expert estimates placed total implementation costs at over $10 billion (potentially $20+ billion using Infoway's own calculations)

### 7.6 Infoway's Response to the Audit
- Infoway accepted all of Fraser's recommendations and committed to implementing new contracting rules by March 2010
- Board approved revised contracting policies September 23, 2009; implemented October 7, 2009
- Commitments included: monitoring checkpoints in procurement workflow; 90-day advance warning reports for expiring contracts; mandatory documentation for fund releases
- Source: [https://www.ourcommons.ca/Content/Committee/402/PACP/WebDoc/WD4020496/PACP-Sessional-ActionPlans/21-Canada%20Health%20Infoway%20Action%20Plan-e.htm](https://www.ourcommons.ca/Content/Committee/402/PACP/WebDoc/WD4020496/PACP-Sessional-ActionPlans/21-Canada%20Health%20Infoway%20Action%20Plan-e.htm)

## 8. PrescribeIT Cancellation (2026): Summary of Public Accountability Issues

### 8.1 Program Timeline
- **2016:** Budget 2016 allocates $50M; Infoway issues RFP for $40M e-prescribing service
- **September 2016:** TELUS Health's lobbyist Charles Harnick registers for Government Procurement/Health Canada lobby work
- **May 2017:** TELUS Health selected as PrescribeIT technical provider; Budget 2017 adds $300M over 5 years
- **Mid-2017:** Pilot launches in Alberta and Ontario
- **Mid-2018:** National rollout begins
- **2025:** Infoway introduces $0.20/prescription pharmacy fee; adoption collapses
- **February 2026:** Cancellation announced; shutdown date set for May 29, 2026
- **February 17, 2026:** Stuart Barnable (TELUS Health's H+K Strategies consultant lobbyist) registration ends

### 8.2 Scale of Failure
- **$250+ million** spent over 9 years
- **Fewer than 5%** of Canadian prescriptions used PrescribeIT at shutdown
- Most prescriptions still sent by fax or on paper — the technology PrescribeIT was intended to replace
- Annual run rate of **$35 million/year** in recent years with negligible usage

### 8.3 Key Government Quotes
- **Guillaume Bertrand** (Health Minister Marjorie Michel's spokesperson): "Since its introduction, and despite the extensive work of our partner Infoway, the adoption remains below anticipated levels" and cited lack of a "federal-provincial-territorial cost-sharing model"
- **Michael Green** (Infoway CEO): "Canada Health Infoway will continue to maintain the standard to support national consistency and enable provinces, territories, and vendors to adopt and apply it in ways that align with their respective priorities and timelines"

### 8.4 Structural Accountability Concerns
1. **IP ownership by TELUS:** $250M in public funds built a system whose core technology IP belongs to a private corporation. Government has no ability to transfer or re-deploy the platform without TELUS's consent.
2. **Not subject to TB procurement rules:** Because Infoway is not a government department, its contract with TELUS Health was not subject to Treasury Board procurement oversight, ACAN (Advanced Contract Award Notice) requirements, or Buyandsell.gc.ca posting rules
3. **Contribution agreement structure:** Health Canada funds Infoway via contribution agreement rather than contracts, further insulating the TELUS Health sub-contract from federal procurement rules
4. **Failed private-sector buyout:** Government could not simply transfer the service, confirming the IP lock-in with TELUS

### 8.5 Industry Response
- **Canadian Pharmacists Association:** Formally requested Infoway pause the per-prescription fee; escalated to Minister of Health; 65% of surveyed pharmacists said they would stop using PrescribeIT if fee was implemented
- Source: [https://www.pharmacists.ca/news-events/news/cpha-urges-pause-on-prescribeit-transaction-fee-that-will-threaten-the-sustainability-of-canadian-pharmacies/](https://www.pharmacists.ca/news-events/news/cpha-urges-pause-on-prescribeit-transaction-fee-that-will-threaten-the-sustainability-of-canadian-pharmacies/)
