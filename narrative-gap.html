Pillar E · The Meta-Finding

The Narrative Gap

Across every part of this investigation, one pattern keeps repeating: the story we are told diverges, in a consistent direction, from what Canada's own data shows. Sometimes the headline is technically true but misleading; sometimes the cause is mis-stated; sometimes the thing being measured is quietly the wrong thing. Each gap below is documented against primary sources — including the cases where the exaggeration favoured our argument, and we corrected it.

Why a divergence this consistent is itself the finding

Any single gap could be an honest simplification. But the direction is not random: the reassuring number is the one in the headline, the alarming one is the one you have to dig for, and the thing that gets measured and publicised is rarely the thing with the largest measured burden. That systematic tilt — not any one statistic — is the story this page exists to name.

To stay credible we apply the same test to ourselves: we lead with rates and disclose denominators, we use primary sources, we flag contested attributions, and we correct exaggerations even when they support the thesis — the “$5B” case below is one we corrected against our own side. A review that only catches the other side's errors is just the same bias wearing a lab coat.
