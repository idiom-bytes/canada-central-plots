Venture Capital · Q1 2026

Record U.S. Quarter. Structural Canadian Crisis.

In Q1 2026, the US venture market hit its all-time quarterly record ($267B, overwhelmingly driven by AI mega-rounds) while Canada recorded its lowest deal count since 2017 — 104 deals, $936M CAD, and effectively zero growth-stage capital. These are two different stories. This report uses 25 quarters of consistent KPMG/PitchBook data for both countries and disaggregates the signal from the noise.

Sources: NVCA Venture Monitor (Q1-2025 – Q1-2026) · KPMG Venture Pulse Q1-2026 · CVCA Q1-2026 (press) · BDC Canada VC Landscape 2025 · CVCA annual reports

About the "$267B vs $1M" comparison: This compares the US all-stage total against Canada's growth-stage only result — two different denominators. Both numbers are real, but they are not comparable as stated. This report presents all valid apples-to-apples pairings across five stages, two methodologies, and 25 quarters.

Section 5 · Key Findings

What the Numbers Actually Show

Finding 01 · The US Headline Is Structurally Misleading

OpenAI alone raised $122B — 46% of the entire US Q1 total. Strip the top-5 deals (OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Waymo, Databricks) and the US market ran at $71.6B — elevated but not a breakout signal. Deal count fell 15% quarter-over-quarter (4,594 deals vs. the prior quarter). The headline reflects one extraordinary company, not an extraordinary market.

Finding 02 · Canada's Crisis Is Structural, Not Cyclical

Q1-2026: $936M CAD, 104 deals — the lowest deal count since 2017. Three consecutive Q1 declines: 180 deals (2023) → 128 (2024) → 116 (2025) → 104 (2026). Growth-stage capital is effectively gone: one deal, $1M, against a five-year Q1 average of $140M. Q1 is always the softest quarter; the comparison is Q1 vs. Q1. This is not seasonal.

Finding 03 · The Gap Opened in 2024 — Not a Decade Ago

Indexed to 2019 = 100, Canada led the US in 2021 (CA: 235 vs US: 229) and tracked it nearly identically through 2023 (CA: 108 vs US: 107). The divergence opened in 2024 (US: 136, CA: 130) and widened sharply in 2025 (US: 217, CA: 122). A gap that opened in 24 months is a capital-availability policy failure — not a structural destiny.

Finding 04 · The Capital Cliff Is Coming in 2027–2028

Early-stage is genuinely healthy: Seed/Pre-seed up 83% YoY ($185M vs $101M), Early (A–B) up 73% ($651M vs $376M). Canada is producing companies. But without growth capital to graduate into, today's seed cohort faces a capital cliff when they need Series C–E funding in 2027–2028. The crisis is lagged — not yet visible in seed numbers.

Finding 05 · Later-Stage Deal Sizes Have Compressed 50%

Later-stage (C–D) raised $248M in Q1-2026 across 9 deals: an average of $27.6M per deal vs. ~$55M historically. Companies that are getting funded at later stage are raising half as much as two years ago — slowing hiring cycles, product development, and market expansion timelines. The volume is there; the scale is gone.

Finding 06 · Both Headlines Are Single-Company Stories

US: OpenAI's $122B is 46% of the entire US total. Canada: Waabi's ~$750M USD Q1-2026 round (KPMG) likely accounts for 70%+ of the KPMG Canada Q1 total ($1.0B CAD). Ex-Waabi, Canada's remaining 103 deals raised an estimated $180–250M — less than $2.5M per deal on average. Strip the single outlier on each side and the structural story is far starker.

Section 6 · What Should Be Done

Four Actionable Proposals

The growth-stage gap opened in 24 months. It can be closed. These proposals are grounded in precedent — not invented — and each targets a specific finding above.

01

VCCI-3 WITH A MANDATORY GROWTH-STAGE ALLOCATION

What: Commit $2B to a third Venture Capital Catalyst Initiative in Budget 2027, with an explicit mandate that 60% of deployment targets growth-stage rounds (Series C–E, >$20M per deal). Neither VCCI-1 ($450M, 2017) nor VCCI-2 ($1B, Budget 2024) carried a growth-stage requirement — both targeted early stage. The fund-of-funds model is proven; what's missing is the mandate.

Precedent: VCCI-1 generated $1.6B in total VC deployment from a $450M government commitment — a 3.6× multiplier. Addresses Finding 02 and Finding 04 directly.

Who implements: ISED Canada via BDC partnership (Budget appropriation — no new legislation required).

Measurable target: 25 growth-stage Canadian deals per year (≥$20M each) by 2028, up from 1 deal in Q1 2026.

02

PENSION HOME-BIAS CO-INVESTMENT TAX CREDIT

What: Introduce a 15% upfront income tax credit for Canadian pension plans (CPPIB, OMERS, OTPP, PSP) that co-invest alongside a qualified lead VC in Canadian growth rounds (Series C–E). Canadian pensions manage $2.5 trillion; a 0.04% domestic growth VC allocation = $1B per year.

Precedent: UK Enterprise Investment Scheme (30% income tax relief) grew UK VC from £600M (2010) to £6.4B (2022). Quebec's LSVCC tax credit structure generates $800M+/year in Quebec VC annually. Addresses Finding 02 and Finding 03.

Who implements: Finance Canada, Income Tax Act amendment.

Measurable target: $500M/year in pension-backed Canadian growth VC by 2027.

03

SCALE BDC DIRECT GROWTH EQUITY TO $300M/YEAR

What: Direct BDC to allocate $300M/year to direct growth equity in Canadian companies at Series C–E, with a 1:1 private capital co-investment requirement. BDC already has a growth equity program — scale it with a specific annual target and a co-investment condition to maximize private leverage.

Precedent: BDC deployed $200M in growth equity in 2022 (BDC Annual Report 2022). Israel's Yozma program (1993) used 8 government-backed funds totalling $100M to catalyze $2.3B in private VC within 10 years. Addresses Finding 02 and Finding 05.

Who implements: BDC Board + Treasury Board direction (no new legislation required).

Measurable target: 20 growth-stage deals ≥$25M each per year in Canada by 2026.

04

REQUIRE PUBLIC QUARTERLY VC STAGE DATA

What: Require CVCA — which receives federal funding through VCCI — to publish stage-level VC investment data quarterly, publicly, within 60 days of quarter end, matching the detail and accessibility of the US NVCA Venture Monitor. The CVCA Q1-2026 full report was not publicly available; data came only from press releases and media reports.

Precedent: NVCA publishes full stage breakdown, deal count, and median deal size each quarter, freely, within 30 days. UK's British Business Bank publishes an annual Small Business Finance Markets report with full stage and regional breakdowns. Addresses Finding 06.

Who implements: ISED Canada, via VCCI funding conditions (contractual, not legislative).

Measurable target: CVCA quarterly stage report publicly available within 60 days of quarter end, starting Q2-2026.

Section 7 · Methodology & Sources

Data Notes

Why KPMG for Quarterly Trend Charts

The quarterly trend and normalized index charts require a continuous, gap-free series with identical methodology for both countries. CVCA (authoritative for Canada) is missing Q2–Q4 2025. NVCA doesn't publish pre-2025 quarterly data publicly. KPMG/PitchBook provides 25 unbroken quarters for both US and Canada from the same source. Mixing CVCA and KPMG would introduce a ~15% step-change; mixing NVCA and KPMG would introduce a 39%+ divergence for some quarters. All absolute dollar figures in text and stat chips use CVCA (Canada) and NVCA (US).

CVCA vs KPMG (Canada)

CVCA = member-reported data, broader coverage (captures smaller seed/angel deals disclosed directly by fund managers). KPMG = PitchBook global deal database, ~10–15% lower than CVCA for the same periods. For Q1-2026: CVCA press = $936M; KPMG = $1,017M. Both are medium confidence (CVCA PDF not publicly released; KPMG is chart read). Use CVCA for narrative text; KPMG for trend charts.

NVCA vs KPMG (United States)

NVCA/PitchBook Venture Monitor and KPMG Venture Pulse both use PitchBook data but with different deal-inclusion filters at the global aggregation level. Example divergence: NVCA Q1-2025 = $91.5B, KPMG Q1-2025 ≈ $56.0B (39% gap). NVCA is authoritative for US; KPMG series used only in trend charts for methodological parity with Canada. This means the KPMG "US" bars are not NVCA figures — see chart notes for details.

Index Methodology & Exchange Rate

Annual index: value / 2019-annual-base × 100. US base = $156.5B USD (NVCA 2019). Canada base = $6.55B CAD (average of CVCA $6.5B and BDC $6.6B). Quarterly index: value / (annual-base ÷ 4) × 100. US quarterly base = $39.125B. Canada quarterly base = $1,637.5M CAD. This keeps annual and quarterly series on the same scale. Exchange rate: CAD/USD = 0.72 (approximate Q1-2026 rate). Used only where explicit USD comparisons are made.

Stage Definitions

CVCA: Pre-seed (<$2M), Seed ($2–5M), Early (Series A–B), Later (Series C–D), Growth (Series E+). This report maps these to NVCA/PitchBook equivalents: Pre-seed/Seed → Seed, Early → Early-stage VC, Later → Late-stage VC, Growth → Venture Growth. US stage estimates: Seed (~$10B estimated); Early ($25.1B from NVCA); Later+Growth ($222.4B headline, $33.8B ex-top-5 estimated). Estimated values marked.
